## Agent tools

**Runta/Baseten FrontierHarness v1.1: 27,280 runs say the best coding-agent harness depends on which model you run** — event date: Tuesday, Nov 17, 2026 (published before 09:53 ET HN front-page post).
Headline results: each model's best-value harness was different — Codex CLI for GLM-5.3, Pi for GLM-5.3-Flash, ZCode for DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, Oh My Pi for Muse, Alma for Kimi-K3 — and with the model fixed, the most expensive harness cost 1.8–5.0× the cheapest per task; Codex CLI swung from cost-rank 1st (GLM-5.3) to 16th (Kimi-K3). Across all models, OpenCode v2 was cheapest ($0.561/task) and ZCode passed most (61.9%). 64.5% of total spend went to runs that did not pass, and 68–82% of failed runs ended with a confident final answer, not a timeout. For harness developers, the study's blunt advice: cut steps, not prompt tokens — steps re-send the whole context and predicted cost per pass when prompt size, tool count and cache hit rate did not.
*Why it matters:* "which harness?" has become a first-order tuning axis next to "which model?" for agent stacks — and for self-hosters this is the first large public grid of harness economics on open-weight models specifically.
*Local inference:* all five models tested are open weights that run locally; the pass-rate spread of 54–69% on the same DeepSeek model across harnesses means harness choice is worth more than a quantization step.
Source: "Introducing FrontierHarness Eval v1.1," Runta blog, Nov 17, 2026 — https://runta.com/blog/introducing-frontierharness-eval-v1-1/
Source: Hacker News discussion, Nov 17, 2026 09:53 ET — https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50532191

## AI security and governance

**Jensen Huang's first statement since resigning: "I own my mistakes" — and he'd be "honored" to return; House panels set Dec. 1** — event date: Tuesday, Nov 17, 2026 (statement published ~11:00 ET morning).
Update on previously reported (ledger: megaspeed-alice-huang cascade). In his first public remarks since resigning Thursday, Huang said he "did not live up to the standards expected of everyone at Nvidia, and I have made serious mistakes in my personal life," added he "created problems for the Board that I deeply regret" and is "committed to cooperating" with the independent investigation — and said that if the board decides it is in the best interest of shareholders, employees, the company and America, "I would be honored" to return as CEO. Nvidia's board is reportedly not considering reinstating him in any role (CNN/CBS sources), even as the reinstatement petition topped 190,000 signatures including several thousand Nvidia employees; President Trump said Monday he "would not mind" a return and floated Tim Cook as a successor. The same morning, House Foreign Affairs, Intelligence and China Select committee chairs announced Huang voluntarily agreed to transcribed interviews on Dec. 1, with Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter following Dec. 2 — after Rep. Mast had threatened a subpoena. Analysts read the sequencing against a comeback: the board's first act this week was standing up a National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee. NVDA closed down 1.7% at $153.51, ~16% below pre-scandal levels; FT also reported Madison Huang helped steer the company's Megaspeed response and temporarily sidelined a comms executive.
*Why it matters:* the governance question is now two-track — whether a founder can return after a national-security-linked scandal, and whether Congress's Megaspeed probe (DOJ-led, per joint DOJ/BIS language) reshapes export-control enforcement across the AI chip supply chain.
