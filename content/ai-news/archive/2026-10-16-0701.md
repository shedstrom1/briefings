## Models and local inference

### NVIDIA releases open Nemotron 3.5 Super VL and Bolt edge models — October 15, 2026

NVIDIA announced Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, an open 120-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts vision-language model built with the Nemotron Coalition for always-on agents. It activates 12 billion parameters per token, carries a 1-million-token context window, and was trained on roughly 26 trillion tokens with about 3.8 million post-training samples. Super VL is available now on Hugging Face, OpenRouter, ModelScope, and as an NVIDIA NIM; vLLM and SGLang both published day-0 support on October 15.

Why it matters: the largest open-weight multimodal release of the window, explicitly pitched at local and desktop deployment — organizations can post-train it on their own data and run it on their own infrastructure, and customers including CrowdStrike, Perplexity, and Salesforce are already customizing it.

### IBM releases Granite Switch 4.1: chat, decisions, and speech from one 8B/30B endpoint — October 15, 2026

IBM Research released Granite Switch 4.1, open models in 8-billion and 30-billion-parameter sizes that compose a Granite base model with a general-purpose Decision aLoRA, a Guardian safety aLoRA, and the Granite Speech 5.0 Turbo-CTC automatic-speech-recognition encoder — all behind one endpoint. The adapters reuse the base model's KV cache, so switching between chat, decision, and transcription tasks adds almost no memory overhead; IBM reports Granite-Switch-4.1-30B completed a five-step RAG workflow faster than a 27-billion-parameter competitor.

Why it matters: modular adapter composition lets teams run routing, safety screening, and transcription on a single deployment instead of maintaining several models.

## Agent tools

### Microsoft Agent Framework 1.22.0 — October 15, 2026

Microsoft released version 1.22.0 of its Python Agent Framework SDK (the .NET line is at 1.20.0, dated October 2). The release adds Python 3.15 support with package-level caveats, configurable service URLs for the Ollama and OpenAI chat clients, and OAuth consent surfacing in non-streaming Foundry responses. It also introduces breaking changes: consolidated content identity, hosted execution, replay, and stream-merge semantics across providers and adapters; fail-closed AG-UI request trust boundaries; and a persisted-state compatibility warning — pre-1.22 runtimes cannot read new records containing `hosted` or `merge_mode`, so old and new workers must not share affected state stores during a rolling deployment.

Why it matters: teams running multi-agent orchestration on Microsoft Agent Framework need a coordinated upgrade path; the breaking changes touch how tools, streams, and approvals are identified across providers including A2A, AG-UI, Anthropic, Bedrock, Claude, Gemini, Mistral, Ollama, and OpenAI.

Sources:
