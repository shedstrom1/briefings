## Models and local inference

**OpenAI claims ω = 9/4 — the biggest matrix-multiplication bound move since 1979**
*Event date: Tuesday, Nov 24, ~7:04 PM ET (repo commit 2026-11-25T00:04:41Z; HN front page 8:19 PM ET).*
OpenAI's `openai/math` repository added a manuscript titled "The matrix multiplication exponent is nine-fourths," claiming that ω is exactly 9/4 (2.25) **over every field** — pairing an upper bound (n×n matrices multipliable with exponent 9/4) with a matching lower bound that formal arithmetic circuits need at least ⌈n^2.25/2⌉ nonscalar multiplication/division gates. The previous best upper bound was ≈2.371177 (August 2026), so the jump is roughly 0.12 in the exponent — not a rounding-error refinement. It extends OpenAI's October 2 preprint (ω ≤ 9/4 over the complex numbers, Lean-checked) by adding the lower bound and generalizing across fields, with geometric/combinatorial obstruction arguments and AI assistance in discovery; Lean verification is reported for division-free circuits over all fields. The paper is not peer reviewed and specialists are already picking through the lemmas.
*Why it matters:* this is a frontier lab shipping a theory-of-computation result that human complexity theorists failed to close for decades, and doing it in a form (Lean-checked) that can be machine-audited rather than merely trusted. Practical fast-matmul code is a long way from an exponent bound — asymptotics don't beat tuned kernels on real silicon by themselves — but the item matters twice over: the result itself, and the signal about where frontier-lab compute is being pointed.
*Local inference:* no hardware or serving claims in the paper; nothing here changes consumer GPU sizing.

**NVIDIA releases open weights for Nemotron-Labs-Image-30B-A3B**
*Event date: weights landed on Hugging Face overnight (repo lastModified 2026-11-25T03:19Z ≈ 10:19 PM ET Nov 24; community quant by 2:10 AM ET, demo Space by 3:53 AM ET).*
The 30B-total / 3B-active MoE is the raster-generation component of NVIDIA's **Nemotron-Labs-VisualCreation** ("Pixel as Code") framework: it targets photorealistic images *and* editable artifacts (webpages, slides, documents, charts, scientific figures) through a generate → render → visually-inspect loop. It is built on the Nemotron-H hybrid Mamba-2 + attention architecture, initialized from Nemotron 3 Nano, trained with progressive mid-training plus GRPO, and ships with its bundled ~642M-parameter L24SQ visual tokenizer (16×16 patches, 196,560-vector Leech-lattice codebook). Reported scores: GenEval 92.86, LongTextBench 97.98, CVTG-2K 92.53, WISE Verified 91.50, BizGenEval 86.0 — versus GPT Image 2's 89.00 / 96.00 / 90.03 / — / 83.2. It is *not* a clean win: GenEval2 65.43 against GPT Image 2's 81.00, and Qwen-Image-Bench 52.46 against 65.23. The harness and the VisionCode models are withheld "upon legal clearance," and the reference inference setup is four H100 80GB GPUs.
*Why it matters:* it extends NVIDIA's open-weight sweep from text/ASR into agentic visual creation, and the tokenizer latency numbers (encoding 41.3 ms, decoding 37.1 ms at batch 1 on an A100 — 7.1×/10.9× faster than Emu3.5) matter more for production loops than the headline benchmark rows do. It is also a reminder to read NVIDIA's benchmark tables per-column: it leads on structured text-in-image tasks and loses the compositional-following column.
*Local inference:* 3B active parameters is a consumer-friendly decode budget, and a community NVFP4 build (`HDiffusion/Nemotron-Labs-Image-30B-A3B-NVFP4`) appeared within four hours of the weights.

## Agent tools

**OpenClaw v2026.11.10: Deep Research on by default, Knowledge vaults, browser handoff**
*Event date: Wednesday, Nov 25, 00:42 EST (release published 2026-11-25T05:42:47Z).*
This is a large release — 7,671 merged PRs, 633 direct commits, 349 contributors. Headline changes: **Deep Research** ships enabled-by-default on the portable engine using your existing model and web tools (an opt-in Google-managed research engine is also available), producing cited reports with side-by-side quotes when sources conflict and an explicit "Unresolved" section, with eligible jobs resuming across Gateway restarts and saved reports kept for seven days. **Knowledge** is a new opt-in memory provider that turns conversations, meetings and files into a linked local Markdown vault with project Timelines and scribes (including Granola ingestion); automatic recall starts in shadow mode, and keyword scoring in shadow mode does send your message plus candidate excerpts to the configured Decision provider. **Browser handoff** lets you take control of an agent-driven tab at a sign-in wall, 2FA prompt or CAPTCHA and release control (auto-return after five idle minutes). Quick Start can now leave the Gateway running as a background service, Copilot joins the default provider list, and guided setup no longer falsely reports "no AI access" when Ollama or LM Studio is running with no model loaded.
*Why it matters:* the default-on research loop is the clearest sign yet that multi-source synthesis is being commoditized into agent harnesses rather than sold as a separate cloud product — and the privacy surface moves accordingly (shadow-mode recall making provider calls is exactly the kind of default a local-first user should audit).
*Local inference:* the release notes are explicit that Deep Research runs on the portable engine with your own model, so this is usable fully off-cloud; Ollama/LM Studio detection fixes also lower the friction of OpenClaw-on-a-workstation setups.

**Amazon and Meta in talks to end the Muse shopping blockade**
*Event date: Wednesday, Nov 25, 3:01 AM ET (Fortune exclusive).*
Update on the Muse-vs-Amazon standoff first reported here under the bank/retailer agent-block story (Nov 22): the two companies have met multiple times about reconnecting Meta's Muse agent to Amazon's storefront, with **search results and advertising** the central unresolved items. Amazon's concerns are laid out plainly — lost ad revenue (Muse bypasses on-site ads), the quality of product results Muse would surface, and the risk that third-party agents dilute Amazon's direct relationship with shoppers. Nothing is agreed, discussions are described as fluid, and any deal is expected to need sign-off from both CEOs. Context Fortune supplies: 10M+ US/Canada Muse setups as of October, Amazon's own "Alexa for shopping" used by 400M+ customers in the past year, Meta's earlier 2023 in-app Amazon purchasing deal, and Morgan Stanley's $190–385B agentic-shopping projection for US e-commerce by 2030.
*Why it matters:* this is the first concrete negotiation over how an agent gets access to a walled storefront, and the settlement terms (who controls ranking, who gets ad attribution) will be the template for every other agent-vs-platform fight. It lands right before Black Friday/Cyber Monday, with roughly half of holiday shoppers expected to use AI to shop (NRF).
*Local inference:* none claimed; this is a distribution fight, not a model or runtime story.

## AI security and governance

**FinCEN discloses a breach and takes systems offline**
*Event date: Tuesday, Nov 24 (FinCEN alert; The Verge update Wed 01:00 UTC).*
The Treasury's Financial Crimes Enforcement Network said it is investigating "a cybersecurity incident that resulted in unauthorized access to a limited subset of its systems," and has taken some systems offline as a precaution. It has not named the actor or scoped the intrusion; Bank Secrecy Act e-filings remain available, some services are degraded, and the Financial Institutions Hotline was unaffected. The disclosure follows the *New York Times* report earlier Tuesday that the CIA and ODNI found a breach of an open-source-intelligence analysis system (with NGA also impacted, per DoD's no-comment), and last week's FBI breach of a warrant/background-check database shared with local law enforcement — no link between them has been established.
*Why it matters:* FinCEN holds suspicious-activity reports and transaction intelligence; a confirmed federal intrusion into a Treasury bureau days after an OSINT-system breach at the agencies that watch for exactly this suggests the "no systemic attack" line from Washington is not matching the incident log. For the AI angle: the suspected vector in the OSINT case is agentic tooling, so a second agency intrusion in the same news cycle feeds the argument that AI-assisted reconnaissance is now ambient.

**EU proposes a €50B Sovereign Tech Equity Fund for AI and quantum**
*Event date: Wednesday, Nov 25 — FT report ~00:40 ET, Commission confirmation same morning (Virkkunen, in Strasbourg).*
The European Commission proposed a €50B (~$55B) **Sovereign Tech Equity Fund** that would take direct equity stakes in European AI and quantum companies, plus procurement preferences for European firms — with the legal basis sitting inside the draft Quantum Act presented the same day. The stated logic is to reduce dependence on US and Chinese technology and stop strategic startups from moving abroad or being bought, given the EU captures only ~5% of global VC. It would dwarf existing vehicles: the €5B Scaleup Europe Fund (run by EQT, first investment Finnish satellite maker ICEYE, targeted to grow to €25B) and the EIB's €70B-by-2027 TechEU push. The proposals need European Council and Parliament approval.
*Why it matters:* state equity in frontier AI is the third model now visibly on the table — US private/neocloud capital, China's state-backed funds, and an EU sovereign-stake vehicle — and procurement preferences plus equity stakes would reshape where European labs and inference providers can be financed and who they can sell to.
*Local inference:* indirect — an equity fund aimed at compute sovereignty is how the EU would subsidize on-continent inference capacity, but no datacenter or GPU line items were announced with it.

**Rackspace kills Rackspace Spot after Kubernetes Secrets breach**
*Event date: Tuesday, Nov 24, 4:37 PM ET (BleepingComputer, from the customer notice).*
Rackspace is permanently discontinuing Spot — its cheap container/Kubernetes platform — on **December 8, 2026 at 5 PM Central**, after intruders used compromised credentials on November 17 to create unauthorized pods inside customer clusters, read Kubernetes Secrets, and list nodes and volumes. Rackspace learned of it on November 18 from a customer report, disabled the admin console, and restored service November 21 after an extended outage; it says the Spot environment is segmented from core infrastructure. All Spot customers are told to treat API tokens, refresh tokens, kubeconfigs and cluster Secrets as compromised regardless of whether they got a direct notification. Published indicators: source IP 23.224.79.106, range 23.224.0.0/15, and pod/object prefixes `i1`, `glm-` and `probe`. Customers are publicly lobbying for Rackspace to reconsider, arguing they'd pay more to keep the service.
*Why it matters:* Spot-style capacity is where hobbyist and small-team AI training/inference lives precisely because it's cheap, and multi-tenant Secrets exposure is the classic path to stolen model-API keys and cloud credentials — this is the same failure shape as the Railway and Northflank incidents, ending in a whole platform's death instead of a migration notice. The `glm-`-prefixed objects are a small but telling fingerprint: commodity attack tooling named after open-weight models.
*Local inference:* if you rent spot GPU/K8s for experiments, the practical instruction is to rotate everything and assume anything stored in a cluster Secret from Nov 16 onward leaked; Rackspace Spot workloads must move by Dec 8.
Source: "Rackspace shuts down Spot cloud platform after security breach" (BleepingComputer, Lawrence Abrams, November 24, 2026, 04:37 PM) — https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/rackspace-shuts-down-spot-cloud-platform-after-security-breach/
