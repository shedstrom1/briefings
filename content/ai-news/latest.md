## AI security and governance

**Anthropic files its S-1: $16.29B H1 revenue, $80.7B loss, Nasdaq "ANT"** — Event date: Monday, Oct 26 (public filing ~9:11 a.m. ET).
The prospectus discloses revenue of $16.29 billion for the six months ended June 30, up 1,304% year-over-year (full-year 2025 was $4.59B), against an H1 net loss of $80.7 billion — a figure driven largely by accounting revaluations of convertible notes, with ~$9B of it compute and infrastructure (FT). Real-world texture: $2.7B of the $3.17B sales-and-marketing spend is channel-partner fees, 48% of revenue flows through AWS, Google Cloud and Azure, debt is $6.2B (Amazon convertibles), and off-balance-sheet commitments ballooned to $265.6B in noncancelable purchase commitments plus $321.4B in undiscounted lease obligations. Governance: the seven co-founders will hold 50.1% of voting power via a single Class F share while the company retains Public Benefit Corporation status; the filing warns a "catastrophic" AI incident's scale of harm could make insurance unavailable (WSJ), flags data-center moratoriums as a risk factor, and warns of "legal liabilities" if its models are used to develop chemical or biological weapons. Reuters reports a majority vote of the co-founders directs the Class F share on key corporate matters; Google also holds an option to buy up to $10B of Class N stock at the IPO price.
*Why it matters:* This is the first full financial teardown of a frontier lab in public markets, and it sets the disclosure bar OpenAI will have to meet. The numbers reframe the "AI business" debate: distribution is rented (48% through hyperscaler marketplaces), compute is leased (~$587B of committed lease/purchase obligations as of mid-year), and mission lock is engineered through a single share.

**Update on previously reported (ledger 2026-10-21, "Meta new non-voting Class C stock to fund AI models"): Meta launches the raise — up to $95B** — Event date: Monday, Oct 26 (CNBC published 7:34 a.m. ET).
Meta said Monday it plans to raise a total of $95 billion to support AI infrastructure and superintelligence: an initial $35B in underwritten public equity offerings, followed by an additional $60B through an at-the-market program. Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom are interested in purchasing up to $5B in shares as cornerstone investors. The capital funds four gigawatt-plus "superclusters," a near-doubling of 2026 infrastructure capacity, and its Muse agent family; Meta's own capex guidance sits at $135–145B for the year, and Goldman Sachs pegs AI-related capex at roughly $7.6T from 2026 through 2031. Shares fell ~1.6% on the news.
*Why it matters:* This executes the Class C non-voting structure reported Oct 21 and makes Meta the third mega AI raise in a month after Alphabet's $85B plan and SpaceX's debuts — a direct stress test of whether public markets keep funding frontier capex.

**Update on previously reported (ledger 2026-10-26 07:00, "Musk: SpaceX closed $40B callable GPU debt"): SEC filing names Apollo; lease targets Vera Rubin** — Event date: Monday, Oct 26 (SEC statement ~8:12 a.m. ET; Bloomberg 9:45 a.m.).
SpaceX filed a binding commitment letter for a master finance lease with an entity managed by Apollo Global Management: Apollo buys GPU pods and related data-center equipment and leases them to SpaceX, with aggregate principal of ~$40B across tranches, each lease running about five to five-and-a-quarter years with quarterly-in-advance rent at roughly one-twentieth of financed cost per tranche. Closing is expected before year-end; lease schedules commence in H1-2027 following GPU delivery, supporting deployment of Nvidia's next-generation Vera Rubin chips.
*Why it matters:* Converts Musk's Saturday X claim into auditable terms and confirms the counterparty, structure and chip target — equipment-backed GPU financing is now a repeatable capital-markets instrument for AI buildout (the same format Bloomberg says is tracking toward a $1T market).
