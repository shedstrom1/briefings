## AI security and governance

**Banks crack down on third-party AI agents — bank terms updated with "at your own risk" language.** Event date: November 22, 2026 (Wired, Nov 22, 6:00 AM). Major US financial institutions are banning third-party bots from logging into accounts: Wells Fargo is set to enact an updated agreement with millions of customers starting Tuesday — "Your use of any AI Agent is AT YOUR OWN RISK" — and, per Wired's reporting, JPMorgan Chase changed terms in late October to ban third-party agents that log in with your credentials; Bank of America warned customers about sharing login info with agents the same day; Citibank and PNC have also changed terms; American Express is looking at limited reimbursement for agentic mistakes. Amazon has blocked Meta's Muse (the widely-used agent) from making purchases, and United Airlines announced that personal bots would be blocked from purchasing flights. Simultaneously, banks are building first-party agent tooling: Wells Fargo's agent-backed assistant has been used over a billion times; Bank of America revamped its EricaAssist agent; Citigroup launched an internal agent-centric development platform.
Why it matters: the "consumer authorizes goals, not every click" problem is now being priced by the industry's most risk-averse players at the point of account access — a policy boundary set by banks each of which has largely avoided public AI-agent coverage until now, and whose first-party-vs-third-party stance signals where agent-account integrations will be allowed.
Source: https://www.wired.com/story/banks-are-cracking-down-on-ai-agents/ (Wired, Nov 22, 2026)

**CVE-2026-134540 — llama.cpp 0.7.0-dev llama-server input-validation flaw (MEDIUM 5.5).** Event date: November 21, 2026 (NVD published Nov 21). A security vulnerability was detected in ggml-org llama.cpp 0.7.0-dev's common_sampler_init (common/sampling.cpp); manipulation of the argument grammar_triggers[].type leads to improper input validation — attack is remote. The exploit is publicly disclosed; the patch upstream landed in b12405 (name: 9f26fe11c73c7e74a5e99e7f1b6370ad8e1cb22d). CVSS 4.0: 5.5 MEDIUM, vector AV:N/AC:L/AT:N/PR:N/UI:N/VC:N/VI:N/VA:L/SC:N/SI:N/SA:N/E:P (CNA VulDB).
Why it matters: anyone running llama-server without the b12405 fix has a publicly disclosed remote-triggerable flaw.
Source: https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2026-134540 (NVD, Nov 21, 2026)

**CVE-2026-134293 — SurrealDB MCP server SQL injection (HIGH 8.8).** Event date: November 21, 2026 (NVD published Nov 21). SurrealDB 3.1.0 before 4.0.0 contains an SQL injection in its Model Context Protocol server: the identifier validator (consume_backtick_ident and validate_identifier) treats the first backtick as the closing delimiter, ignoring escape characters SurrealQL's parser honors. Attackers supplying MCP tool arguments with an escaped backtick can break out of the quoted identifier and execute arbitrary SurrealQL. Fixed in 4.0.0 (semver). CVSS 4.0: 8.8 HIGH (CNA VulnCheck), vector AV:N/AC:L/AT:N/PR:N/UI:N/VC:H/VI:L/VA:N/SC:N/SI:N/SA:N.
Why it matters: the MCP surface is exactly where local/agent-connected agents will keep calling tools; a high-severity, pre-auth identifier-escape in the MCP layer is the kind of flaw worth patching across any agent that shells out to SurrealDB.
Source: https://nvd.nist.gov/vuln/detail/CVE-2026-134293 (NVD, Nov 21, 2026)
