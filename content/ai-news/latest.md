**vLLM v0.32.0 brings NVFP4 KV cache to consumer Blackwell** — event: Sunday, October 18 (release published 06:50 UTC / 2:50 a.m. ET).
The release bundles 814 commits from 363 contributors. Highlights: NVFP4 KV-cache now runs on SM8x and SM12x GPUs (with XQA decode on SM12x), GLM-5.x defaults to FP8 KV cache on SM100 (roughly halving KV memory per rank at a 7–11% TTFT cost in the PR's benchmark), a new `POST /v1/systemone` endpoint that answers structured choice, noul and score questions over a structured state, a generic `hf` tool-and-reasoning parser that reads a checkpoint's `response_template` (no model-specific parser needed), multi-API-server connection routing for 43–85% more requests/sec on short requests, and new model support including MiniCPM-V 4.7, Nemotron 3.5 ASR and EmbeddingGemma 2.
*Why it matters:* KV cache is the memory wall for long agentic sessions; getting FP4-class KV compression onto SM12x means the same VRAM budget serves longer contexts on the GPUs people actually own at home.
Source: vLLM GitHub Releases, v0.32.0, published 2026-10-18T06:50:48Z — https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/releases/tag/v0.32.0

## Agent tools

**Replit makes OpenAI its default provider over Anthropic** — event: TechCrunch Disrupt remarks Thursday, October 15; CEO clarifications to TechCrunch over the weekend (article URL-dated October 17, updated with weekend comments).
CEO Amjad Masad said Replit is "no longer working with Anthropic," citing cost, partnership and performance — Replit's internal and public benchmarks allegedly favor OpenAI's Sol and Astra for building vibe-coded apps from scratch. He later clarified that OpenAI is now the *default* for Replit Agent while Claude models remain selectable, which still shifts the bulk of spend since most users keep the default. Replit had been a top-five Anthropic customer; people familiar with the Anthropic partnership disputed that it had ended, and Replit's own September blog posts still cited Claude Fable 5/5.1 in production. Masad also said enterprise went from ~2% of revenue last year to ~15% this year, with more than half expected from enterprise and government in 2027.
*Why it matters:* a top-five Anthropic customer flipping its default on cost/performance is a concrete data point in the frontier-model pricing war — and the disputed framing shows how noisy these provider-vendor announcements are.

## AI security and governance

**OpenCode breach exposed stored provider API keys; company blames a "distillation attack"** — event: Saturday, October 17 (email to affected users Saturday morning; public statement October 17; HN front page Saturday 2:01 p.m. ET, 903 points).
OpenCode — the open-source, model-agnostic coding agent (terminal, desktop, IDE) from the Anomaly Innovations team — disclosed unauthorized access to a legacy system it was already preparing to decommission. Third-party provider keys stored in affected Console workspaces, including OpenAI, Anthropic and Google keys, may have been exposed; the affected set is ~1.4% of Console users who had linked external providers. OpenCode emailed affected users and told the rest they were not impacted, urged immediate revoke-and-rotate plus usage/billing review, said it is working with the three labs, and characterized the incident as tied to a large-scale distillation attack — while leaving the entry point, dwell time, key count and misuse status unexplained. It said the incident remained ongoing at publication; some users report usage charges they did not make.
*Why it matters:* this is the credential-store failure mode of the agent era — the coding agent's console is now where your frontier-model keys live, and the stated motive (mass unauthorized model-output harvesting to distill rival capability) reframes a credential breach as a model-data heist. If you run OpenCode Console with linked providers, rotate keys now and audit billing; a fuller technical report is still outstanding.

**Meta rewrote its scraping clause — legal experts say the problem is still there** — event: Saturday, October 17 (Nieman Lab follow-up published 2:49 p.m. ET; Meta statement issued Saturday).
After Nieman Lab's Thursday exposé that Meta's new commercial terms (effective October 30, no opt-out for business accounts) would let Meta crawl sites "associated with" business accounts and use scraped content to *train* its AI products, Meta revised the language Friday afternoon: "train" is gone and permitted uses are narrowed to "advertising and business products." But lawyers told Nieman Lab the rewrite still permits broad scraping that could supersede robots.txt — "develop any business product" is undefined, "develop" plausibly includes "train," and no robots.txt respect was actually written into the terms. Meta's Saturday statement insists robots.txt preferences have always been respected and "has not changed," while the revised text contains no robots.txt language.
*Why it matters:* for publishers this is a licensing-fee dodge by contract rather than crawler; for the ecosystem it's another case (see OpenCode, OpenAI's disclosure page) of AI data practices being contested in fine print and court filings rather than product UI. The terms still take effect October 30.
