*Why it matters:* the black-market curve is the cleanest read on how binding advanced-node supply really is, and it is accelerating rather than stabilising. Sustained $3M B300 pricing is what pushes Chinese labs toward domestic silicon and open-weight efficiency work, and every restriction-evasion channel exposed here raises the odds of further enforcement tightening that also hits legitimate pro-consumer cards.
*Local inference:* the two cards named on the stalls — RTX 5090 (32GB) and RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell (96GB) — are exactly the parts people are pricing for serious local rigs, and their street pricing tracks the same supply squeeze described here.

**China's memory makers post record results — and still cannot ship HBM in volume before 2028** — Event date: Wednesday, Nov 25, 2026 (published 13:00 UTC / 08:00 EST).
Tom's Hardware's audit of China's DRAM and NAND industry puts **CXMT at ¥132.0B (~$19.6B) nine-month net profit** versus a ¥5.28B loss a year earlier, on **¥269.0B (~$40.0B) revenue — more than 8x growth**, and **YMTC at ¥47.04B revenue and ¥33.38B profit in Q1 alone, more than double 2025**. CXMT is now the **fourth-largest DRAM maker at 9.5% of Q2 revenue share** (TrendForce), runs three ~100k-wafer/month fabs, has LPDDR6 in **mass production for Xiaomi**, and says its first **4F² server DDR5 RDIMM lands by year-end** on DDR5-8000/24Gb dies. YMTC holds ~200k wpm in Wuhan with Xinzhou Fab 3 due at 50k wpm in 2027 (about half earmarked DRAM) and claims **14% of global NAND revenue share**. The hard limit is HBM: CXMT's IPO prospectus **lists no HBM project**, its HBM3E is only "in risk production", a Shanghai HBM packaging plant is due end-2026, and Huawei designs its own HBM (HiZQ 2.0 on the Ascend 950DT) without naming a fab — Tom's is "skeptical of volume HBM from a Chinese fab … before 2028." Meanwhile SK hynix says its DRAM and HBM are effectively **sold out through 2029**, and China Flash Market reports this week's SSD prices falling with **spot NAND wafers more than 30% below official pricing**.
*Why it matters:* memory, not logic, is the binding cost of every local-AI machine right now — 128GB unified boxes, DGX Spark-class parts and multi-DIMX inference rigs all price off DRAM. China adding capacity and shipping DDR5-8000/4F² parts is the main route to relief, while the HBM gap guarantees datacentre accelerator scarcity keeps bleeding into consumer pricing.
*Local inference:* CXMT's 24Gb DDR5 dies and LPDDR6 output are the specific parts that would ease the RAM crunch behind 64–128GB local builds; the absence of domestic HBM is why B-series/MI-series pricing stays elevated and why the community keeps pushing host-RAM/SSD expert-offload engines instead.

**Samsung and SK hynix cross-license YMTC's Xtacking stacking patents** — Event date: Wednesday, Nov 25, 2026 (Nikkei Asia, cited by Yonhap Infomax).
Nikkei Asia, citing three sources briefed on the matter, reports that **Samsung Electronics and SK hynix have gained access to elements of YMTC's Xtacking 3D-NAND method through cross-licensing agreements**. Xtacking manufactures memory cells and the controlling CMOS logic on separate wafers and bonds them; YMTC unveiled it in 2018 and has shipped it in volume since 2019, and says it has filed **12,000+ patents worldwide, including 4,000+ in the U.S. since 2018**. Samsung declined to comment on the deal but confirmed bonding "enables higher memory density"; SK hynix and YMTC declined. Counterpoint puts YMTC at **14% of Q2 NAND shipments, up from 11% a year earlier**, level with Kioxia behind Samsung (28%), SK hynix (19%) and Micron (15%); YMTC also filed for an IPO via parent Changjiang Cunchu Holdings in August. One industry executive told Yonhap the real motive is defensive: cross-licensing "to avoid legal disputes, as YMTC has already filed many patents related to stacking technology."
*Why it matters:* this is the clearest evidence yet that a Chinese memory company is setting terms with the industry leaders rather than licensing from them — the same status flip that open-weight labs just went through, in a market with far higher capital barriers.
*Local inference:* bonding-driven density growth plus this week's NAND spot collapse (>30% below official pricing, per Tom's) is the most direct route to cheaper multi-terabyte model and KV-cache storage tiers for local rigs.
