## AI hardware and silicon

### Lambda lights the first production co-packaged-optics AI factory — 10,000+ Blackwell Ultra GPUs on NVIDIA Photonics switches
**Event date: November 16, 2026** (SC26, St. Louis). Lambda's press release (Business Wire, dateline San Francisco, November 16, 2026) disclosed what it calls the world's first large-scale deployment of co-packaged optics — NVIDIA Photonics switches wiring a 10,000+ GPU NVIDIA Blackwell Ultra (GB300) cluster at Lambda's Quebec AI factory. The switches carry 144 ports at 800 Gb/s each, are liquid-cooled, and use 18 removable external light sources instead of one laser per port. Lambda's benchmarking reports a 3.8% network-latency reduction at rack scale rising to 10.1% at pod scale with identical application throughput, median optics temperature of 36 °C versus 51 °C for pluggables, up to 15x higher optics reliability, up to 5x better optics power efficiency, and 4x fewer lasers. The campus itself is ~142 MW of nearly 100% renewable power with free-air cooling available roughly 80% of the year at a guaranteed PUE of 1.2. ServeTheHome's Patrick Kennedy published his analysis at 4:24 PM ET today — inside this window — noting the optics sit directly on the switch ASIC so "there are far less failure-prone parts between the optics and the silicon," and that NVIDIA Photonics ships Quantum-X (first-gen) and Spectrum-X (second-gen) CPO switch lines.
- **Why it matters:** CPO has been a datacenter-roadmap story for three years; this is the first disclosure of it carrying production traffic at ten-thousand-GPU scale, and the reported 36 °C optics temperature is the number to watch — thermals, not latency, are what killed earlier CPO attempts. For the local-AI crowd the read-through is indirect but real: every watt NVIDIA saves in the network fabric is watt budget returned to HBM-equipped accelerators, and the same liquid-cooled, low-temp discipline is what makes dense-memory inference boxes viable.
- **Local inference:** No desk-side device ships CPO; this is supply-side news. It targets 800 Gb/s fabric and NVL72-class rack deployments. The homelab takeaway is that 10/40/100GbE remains the correct purchase for multi-node inference for years, since the reliability gap CPO closes is a rack-scale problem.

## Inference software and benchmarks

### vLLM merges LUT-B per-channel block32 weight scales aligned to Rubin's UE8M0 format
**Event date: November 16, 2026** (merged 20:53 UTC / 3:53 PM ET). PR #67267, "[Quant] LUT-B per-channel block32 weight scales," makes vLLM's LUT-B MoE quantization path fit weight scales at the granularity the Rubin (SM107) instruction actually consumes — one UE8M0 exponent per output channel per 32-column block ([E, N, K/32]) — replacing the old scheme of one exponent per 8x64 codebook tile that was repeat-interleaved to the required layout. The author reports 1.09–1.73x lower reconstruction error on synthetic weights at the same codebook size, and notes the SM107 runtime image (7168 B per 128x128 tile) and kernel are unchanged, so there is no runtime memory or speed penalty; only the offline fitting changed. The PR explicitly aligns vLLM with a block32-scale LUT-B checkpoint format (compressed-tensors #996).
- **Why it matters:** Lookup-table weight quantization is the cheap-memory path for MoE experts, and scale granularity is where these schemes lose accuracy. Getting the offline fitter to emit scales in the hardware's native block32/UE8M0 format means quantized checkpoints start shipping in the form the silicon consumes — the same "quantize to the ISA" move that FP4 checkpoints made on Blackwell.
- **Local inference:** Nothing to download yet for 16 GB consumer cards — SM107 is the Rubin datacenter target. The practical signal: LUT-B-style codebook quants on MoE experts are maturing fast, and the same fitter lands in compressed-tensors, which vLLM on RTX 50-class hardware uses directly.
- Source: GitHub — https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/67267 (merged 2026-11-16 20:53 UTC)

### tamserve: one RTX 4090 tracks 64 live camera feeds in 5.7 GB by sharing a single SAM2/EdgeTAM copy
**Event date: November 16, 2026** (Show HN posted 4:14 PM ET, 4 points). Aryaman Darda's tamserve is a pip-installable Apache-2.0 serving engine for Meta's SAM 2 / SAM 2.1 / EdgeTAM segment-anything trackers: instead of one model copy per camera, every camera shares one model with continuous batching — "what vLLM does for chat." README benchmarks on a single RTX 4090: eight cameras in 2.2 GB versus 8.6 GB for stock per-camera copies; 219 frames/s on one camera versus 177; 64 cameras at 321 frames/s in 5.7 GB (stock code would need ~70 GB); an 800-frame video in 0.28 GB instead of 9.8 GB regardless of length; DAVIS 2017 accuracy held at 0.8685 versus 0.8686. A `Tracker` can be prompted by name ("dog", "the red mug") via Meta's SAM 3, and a `Router` spreads cameras over several GPUs.
- **Why it matters:** The interesting idea isn't tracking — it's proof that the continuous-batching/vLLM pattern transfers to any vision model whose reference code is single-stream. That's the same bottleneck that makes local video pipelines VRAM-hungry.
- **Local inference:** Explicitly measured on one RTX 4090 (24 GB); runs on any NVIDIA GPU PyTorch supports (needs bf16 hardware support for sane speed; the README warns the free Colab T4 takes ~3 s per SAM 3 look). Fits comfortably on the 8–16 GB RTX 4060 Ti/5060 Ti class at reduced camera counts; CPU mode exists for experimentation only.
- Source: GitHub — https://github.com/aryamandarda/tamserve
- Source: Hacker News (Show HN, 2026-11-16 21:14:39 UTC, 4 points) — https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50523377

## Model releases (community research)

### "Branchy" training: a transformer whose middle layers split into branches, capping distributed inference at two network hops
**Event date: November 16, 2026** (Reddit post 21:07 UTC / 4:07 PM ET). ManuXD32 — developer of AI Doomsday Toolbox, which already does distributed LLM inference across Android devices — published a week-long experiment in which the model is trained so its middle layers are split into parallel branches instead of a single stack. A coordinator computes embeddings plus a few trunk layers, dispatches to all branches in parallel, collects their results, then runs fusion layers and final blocks. The claim: any number of branches (2 or 8 machines) costs a maximum of two network jumps, and no device sits idle waiting on the last layer slice — attacking the two failure modes (latency and idle devices) that make device-parallel inference get slower as you add hardware. The author reports "the good and the bad" results; the post is an experiment write-up, not a release.
- **Why it matters:** Every real homelab distributed-inference attempt (llama.cpp split layers, Exo-style pipelines, Android phone clusters) is layer-pipelined, so wall-clock time scales with the number of devices. Architecting for branch-parallel inference is the first community attempt I've seen to fix that at training time rather than in the serving layer — if the quality holds, it changes the economics of using a rack of used phones or several 16 GB cards as one machine.
- **Local inference:** Explicitly aimed at multi-device rigs — Android device farms and VPN-joined boxes (the author calls out Tailscale/WireGuard round-trips). A 2-branch version maps onto a 5060 Ti + 4060 laptop pair; nothing to download yet, this is architecture research.
- Source: Reddit r/LocalLLaMA (u/ManuXD32, 2026-11-16 21:07 UTC) — https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLaMA/comments/1xy1b2w/branchy_distributed_inference_focused_training/

### Browser-only video captioning: Whisper-base plus an 11M BERT distilled from a 2B omni model
**Event date: November 16, 2026** (Reddit post 21:40 UTC / 4:40 PM ET). Pantheon3D (the author) released cuecaps.com/studio, a video-captioning tool where nothing runs server-side: transcription is `onnx-community/whisper-base_timestamped` through transformers.js — WebGPU in fp32 on real GPUs, WASM with a q8 decoder elsewhere (software WebGPU adapters proved slower than WASM, so they fall back too) — and the styling tagger is an 11M-parameter BERT-mini, 8 labels per token, ~12 MB, running q8 in WASM. Its training labels were generated by LFM3-Omni-2B on llama.cpp on the author's own PC at about 190 scripts per minute on an RTX 4070 Ti Super with 128 parallel slots, and it only replaced the original hand-written rules after beating them on held-out data. The tagger is English-only, and noisy audio can still be offloaded to a cloud option.
- **Why it matters:** It's a compact worked example of the current local-AI recipe — distill from a big model on your own GPU, ship the student as a 12 MB WASM artifact, keep zero server cost — and the "had to beat hand-written rules on held-out data" bar is the right instinct for these tiny specialist models.
- **Local inference:** Runs on any laptop with a browser; the GPU path is fp32 WebGPU, the fallback is WASM q8. The training teacher (LFM3-Omni-2B on llama.cpp) sized at roughly a 4–6 GB quant — appropriate for 8 GB cards and up.
- Source: Reddit r/LocalLLaMA (u/Pantheon3D, 2026-11-16 21:40 UTC) — https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLaMA/comments/1xy25lw/whisper_base_and_an_11m_param_bert_i_distilled/
