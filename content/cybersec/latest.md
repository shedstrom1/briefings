## Vulnerabilities and patches

**Researcher discloses "God Mode" flaws in six nations' visa and border-control systems** — event date: November 24, 2026 (public disclosure)
Independent researcher Eaton Zveare revealed critical API vulnerabilities — broken object-level authorization (BOLA), broken function-level authorization (BFLA) and missing authentication — in the online passport/visa platforms of Nigeria, Ethiopia, Zambia, Sri Lanka, Fiji and Ghana. The flaws allowed unauthorized access to millions of travelers' visa and passport records (potentially including fingerprints) and, in some systems, administrative functions that approve or deny entry. Zveare found roughly a dozen flaws in Nigeria alone after a Bing search for "admin panel" led him to a private back-end portal; many systems were built by third-party contractors, and he suggests AI-assisted development may have contributed to Nigeria's flaws. CERT responses ranged from excellent (Nigeria, fast remediation) to unresponsive; he contacted contractors directly where needed, and says "everything is pretty much fixed" now.
- **Why it matters:** border-control platforms are national critical infrastructure, and the findings show commodity web flaws — the OWASP top-10 basics — persisting in systems that decide who enters a country, often with no vendor accountable for patching. Zveare will publish full technical detail at Black Hat Europe on December 10.
- **Severity:** Not specified by source (described as critical; no CVSS published).
- **Action:** Organizations or contractors operating similar visa/entry-authorization portals should test for BOLA/BFLA and unauthenticated API endpoints; affected countries' operators should re-verify the fixes, since some were remediated by contractors with no CERT coordination.
- Source: "'God Mode' Flaws Could Wreak Border-Control Havoc" — Dark Reading, November 24, 2026 — https://www.darkreading.com/vulnerabilities-threats/god-mode-flaws-wreak-border-control-havoc

## Attacks and campaigns

**Radware breach: unauthenticated internal Cloud WAF management API exposed customers' cloud log-export keys** — event date: November 24, 2026 (disclosure via BleepingComputer); unauthorized access occurred November 19, 2026
Radware told BleepingComputer in a root-cause analysis report that on November 19 an unauthorized party reached an internal-only Cloud WAF management API that was exposed to the public internet with no authentication. Confirmed potentially accessed data: customer contact details and, for a subset of customers, the credentials configured to export logs into their own storage — Azure Blob Storage access keys or SAS tokens, AWS S3 access keys, or SFTP credentials. Radware detected the breach ten hours after the initial access, blocked the originating IP and revoked internal credentials. WAF traffic inspection, certificates and customer application traffic were not exposed, the company says — but customers have already reported AWS alerts showing an unfamiliar IP attempting to reach their log-export buckets, and Radware has not yet ruled out misuse of the exposed keys.
- **Why it matters:** log-export credentials are a quiet lateral path: write/read keys to a customer's own storage turn a vendor breach into a per-customer cloud incident, and Radware serves 12,500+ enterprise and telecom customers. A security vendor leaving its own management API open without auth is also an accountability story for buyers evaluating AppSec vendors' hygiene.
- **Severity:** Not specified by source (no CVSS; incident still under investigation, no SEC filing yet).
- **Action:** Radware "strongly" advises affected Cloud WAF/Bot Management customers to rotate all log-export credentials now (Azure Blob keys/SAS, AWS S3 keys, SFTP) and audit cloud storage access logs for unexpected access since November 19.
- Source: "Radware discloses breach exposing customer cloud access keys" — BleepingComputer, November 24, 2026, 12:49 PM EST — https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/radware-discloses-breach-exposing-customer-cloud-access-keys/

**New Iran-aligned "Volant Manticore" uses IPFS-based KuboShell backdoor; first Iranian group to make Cyprus a primary target** — event date: November 24, 2026 (Check Point Research disclosure)
Check Point Research documented Volant Manticore, a previously undocumented Iran-nexus espionage group it links to Iran's Ministry of Intelligence and Security, active since September 2025. Its trademark is KuboShell, a 64-bit Windows backdoor that uses IPFS — via the commercial Pinata pinning service with a hard-coded access token — as its C2: victims register by uploading a file named after their MAC address and poll for `<MAC>.txt` command files every 60 seconds, executed through PowerShell. KuboShell works as a guardrail before deploying the Homa Framework, a JavaScript reimplementation of MuddyWater's MuddyC2Go client, via a three-stage chain (PowerShell stager to 185.205.187.109:8000, in-memory loader, JS backdoor). Delivery lures included an AI-generated persona's fake CV targeting a Cyprus-based airline and "AI_Healthcare_Seminar.zip" archives against Saudi hospitals in September, coinciding with Saudi–Houthi escalation. CPR assesses with medium confidence the group is formed of former MuddyWater operators who split around 2023 and now run separate infrastructure.
- **Why it matters:** IPFS-plus-commercial-pinning is a resilient, takedown-resistant C2 pattern that hides inside legitimate decentralized-file traffic, and the shift to healthcare targets during armed conflict echoes MOIS's history of disruptive operations. Cyprus — a hub for Western military and energy ties with Israel and Greece — becoming a dedicated Iranian collection target is a geopolitical signal defenders in Europe and the Gulf should read.
- **Severity:** Not specified by source (nation-state espionage; IOCs published in the report).
- Source: "Volant Manticore: Flying Under the Radar" — Check Point Research, November 24, 2026 — https://research.checkpoint.com/2026/volant-manticore-flying-under-the-radar/
- Second source: "Iran-Aligned Volant Manticore Uses IPFS-Based KuboShell Backdoor in Espionage Campaigns" — The Hacker News, November 24, 2026, 16:54 PM EST — https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/iran-aligned-volant-manticore-uses-ipfs.html

## Defensive tools and advisories

**CISA adds three actively exploited flaws to KEV: Idunno.AntiCsrf, Drupal core, Optimizely** — event date: November 24, 2026 (CISA alert, published after this job's 12:00 PM fire) — *Update on previously reported events*
CISA added CVE-2026-131914 (Idunno.AntiCsrf deserialization RCE in the abandoned ASP.NET anti-CSRF library, CVSS 9.3 — exploitation first reported by The Hacker News November 23, with watchTowr observing in-the-wild redirect-probes this morning), CVE-2026-132165 (Drupal core installer RCE, rated Highly Critical 18/25 by Drupal and fixed in 10.6.22/11.3.22/11.4.13), and CVE-2026-132224 (Optimizely Web Experimentation "external control of critical state data," the signup-flow flaw behind the OPTIMJACK skimming campaign against 178 organizations including BBC, CNN, Zoom and Salesforce). The alert invokes BOD 26-04's risk-based remediation requirements for federal civilian agencies but lists no fixed due dates, and CISA notes KEV additions now require a CVE, exploitation evidence and mitigation guidance.
- **Why it matters:** two of three are on abandoned or lightly-guarded code paths — a retired CSRF library and a CMS installer window — and Optimizely's inclusion signals the U.S. government treats third-party tag compromise as active exploitation, not just a web-defacement nuisance. If you still run any of these stacks, treat KEV status as a same-week patch-or-compensate deadline.
- **Severity (as published):** CVE-2026-131914: CVSS 9.3. CVE-2026-132165: Drupal rating "Highly Critical" (18/25). CVE-2026-132224: Not specified by source.
- **Action:** Federal agencies per BOD 26-04; everyone else — remove Idunno.AntiCsrf.dll entirely (no patch will come; audit web.config, bin/ and IIS logs, rotate config secrets), upgrade Drupal to the fixed releases, and re-audit Optimizely track.js snippets and org access.
- Source: "CISA Adds Three Known Exploited Vulnerabilities to Catalog" — CISA Alert, November 24, 2026 — https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026-11-24/cisa-adds-three-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog
