## Vulnerabilities and patches

**N-able ships Hotfix 3 for N-central with 17 CVEs as miners roll on unpatched servers** — *Event date: Nov 16–17, 2026*
N-able released N-central 2026.4 Hotfix 3, a critical security update covering 17 vulnerabilities (CVE-2026-129271 through CVE-2026-129287) spanning authentication bypass via predictable MMS3 appliance passcode, syslog injection to root command execution, missing authentication on ServerMMS2/ServerMMS tasks, an XXE in custom service import, SSRF in Report Manager/Data Export, a path-traversal RCE in SIS GenericFiles, and a Patch Management LPE (vendor severity ratings per-CVE: Not specified by source). The legacy-API configuration option is now completely removed — closing the mode that the 4EverAble2Shell root-RCE chain depends on — while Remote Control and Intune integration remain blocked; un-upgraded Agents and Probes are "susceptible to impersonation." This continues the Able2Shell saga first disclosed Nov 9 (33 zero-days, PoC-linked scanning, 21 production servers now XMRig-mined).
**Why it matters:** The only durable kill-switch for an actively exploited RMM chain is the vendor deleting the attack surface, and N-able did exactly that mid-incident; MSPs still on HF2 are exposed to a proven root RCE, and agent/probe impersonation means server-side patching alone is not remediation.
**Action:** On-premise customers must upgrade servers immediately and update *all* Agents and Probes to latest; hosted tenants are auto-updated but still must roll agent updates. Keep Remote Control/Intune integrations restricted until N-able restores them.

**ConnectWise closes ScreenConnect probe: extensions disabled across 400 environments, technician clients must be ≥26.6.5** — *Event date: Nov 16, 2026 (investigation completed; continuing attacks)* — *Update on SCKit-ScreenConnect-MSP-campaign / sckit-cve-2026-84869 (ledger Nov 11/12).*
ConnectWise completed its investigation into unauthorized access targeting partner ScreenConnect environments and said attacks on on-premises installations continue wherever the abused third-party extensions (Labtech Plus, RMM Plus, RMM+, RMM Plus Password Link, RMM+ Password Link) remain enabled; the vendor disabled all five across 400 partner environments and told on-prem partners to keep them off. The required technician-client floor, 26.6.5, is the build that fixes CVE-2026-84869 (CVSS 9.9, file transfer/execution through an active session without Host confirmation). Huntress separately reported a renewed SCKit spike on Nov 13 hitting 205 hosts in 41 organizations, with rogue ScreenConnect clients hosted at 185.241.211.197 (bulletproof hosting linked to 1337 Services GmbH) and a "Windows Update Agent" service masquerading under LocalSystem for persistence.
**Why it matters:** This is the worm-style inversion of RMM trust — victim endpoints booby-trapped to infect the *technician* via RelayTrap — and ConnectWise's own guidance confirms disabling extensions and updating "do not remove unauthorized access or persistence already established," so every affected partner owes a full compromise assessment, not just a patch.
**Action:** Verify technician clients ≥26.6.5, keep the five extensions disabled, update on-prem ScreenConnect to latest supported, and hunt rogue clients/persistence (UpdateConnect.ClientService.exe under C:\ProgramData\Windows Update Agent).

## Attacks and campaigns

**OPTIMJACK: Optimizely signup-flow flaw injects skimmer + iOS exploit kits into BBC, CNN, Zoom, Salesforce scripts** — *Event date: Nov 16, 2026 (first injection 04:21 UTC; vendor notice 01:02 UTC Nov 17)*
An attacker exploited a vulnerability in Optimizely Web Experimentation's account signup flow to write malicious loaders into the Project JavaScript of customer projects — still served from cdn.optimizely.com with Optimizely's own certificate, so browsers trusted it. Report URI found injected code in 188 of 1,611 checked snippets, belonging to 178 organizations (Zoom, BBC, CNN, Salesforce, GitLab, IKEA, HSBC, Equifax, Air France-KLM, Ledger, Peacock, UNICEF USA — plus Optimizely's own site); the payload (1688360[.]cc/track.js, in three variants including a Base64-hidden and a fully embedded copy) swapped crypto wallet addresses, skimmed card number/expiry/CVV from site-owned form fields to 1688360[.]cc/card.php, exfiltrated non-HttpOnly cookies plus page screenshots, and on iPhones/iPads delivered the Coruna (iOS 13–17.2.1) and DarkSword (iOS 18.4–18.7) exploit kits — with ~$7,040 in confirmed wallet transfers in nine hours and a Smartsheet re-infection suggesting continued attacker access. Cleanup began ~20:40 UTC; by 21:52 UTC only one snippet remained dirty. Report URI's CSP audit of 256 affected hosts found 68 with no CSP at all and only ~15% fully blocking the payload; Optimizely's docs had recommended 'unsafe-eval'.
**Why it matters:** A third-party tag compromise where the malicious code originates from the *vendor's own trusted CDN* defeats allow-listing and CSP-as-savior thinking — and the campaign is opportunistic-serial (same injector previously planted via Kissmetrics, StorifyMe and Minute Media scripts since late last week), so expect the playbook to repeat on other experimentation/analytics platforms.
**Action:** Audit Project JavaScript and per-experiment custom code for `1688360`, `atob(`, or unknown scripts; rotate account/API-token credentials that could write projects; treat cards entered on those sites Nov 16 as exposed; block 1688360[.]cc, webshell[.]lol and the two .icu iOS-stage domains; update iOS to 18.7.3+/26.3+; deploy strict script-src/connect-src CSP (allowing cdn.optimizely.com does not help).

**Kaspersky: Operation ShadowCloud plants GitDoor/GraphDoor backdoors with GitHub, SharePoint and Ably C2** — *Event date: Nov 16–17, 2026 (research disclosure; campaign live since at least early 2026)*
Kaspersky published a two-part analysis of an active espionage campaign targeting government organizations in Asia and Africa that abuses the legitimate MeshAgent remote-management utility to drop two previously undocumented backdoors — GitDoor (GitHub C2 via the public "Havo" and private "Trix" repos of account LatonyCharnleyaW, created by end-2024) and GraphDoor (Microsoft login via login.microsoftonline.com, commands staged on a SharePoint drive), with modified Erfrp and Ably-based proxy plugins to blend traffic. GitDoor supports 26 commands including CookieKatz browser-cookie dumps, LSASS credential dumping via Mimikatz, keylogging to %APPDATA%\Microsoft\SystemCertificates\Cache\logs.dat, WhatsApp decryption-key theft, NTLM relay and firewall-rule tampering; initial access traces to reuse of a pre-existing network infection, with a hard-coded MeshAgent C2 at 27.102.66[.]162. Kaspersky observed staging-directory overlap with Sophos-tracked Bronze Sandcastle and tactical similarities to FamousSparrow and CopperTurtle/BlackTech, assessing a Chinese-speaking group as likely.
**Why it matters:** Cloud-API C2 is detection-avoidance at its cleanest — GitHub commits and Microsoft Graph calls are indistinguishable from normal enterprise traffic — and the one-day co-deployment windows with known China-nexus groups suggest either collaboration or a shared contractor pool feeding multiple taskings.
**Action:** Hunt for MeshAgent installs you didn't authorize, connections to wss://27.102.66[.]162, and the named GitHub repos; alert on named-pipe "Intel_Log" creation under %APPDATA% and login.microsoftonline.com token use from non-corporate processes.
- Source: The Hacker News — "New GitDoor and GraphDoor Backdoors Turn GitHub and SharePoint Into Covert C2 Channels," Nov 17, 2026 — https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/new-gitdoor-and-graphdoor-backdoors.html
- Source (second): Kaspersky Securelist — "Operation ShadowCloud: initial infection and the GitDoor backdoor" (part 1) and "GraphDoor backdoor, plug-ins and technical attribution" (part 2), Nov 16–17, 2026 — https://securelist.com/operation-shadowcloud-gitdoor/122004/

**Fugaku supercomputer taken offline after status-dashboard hack exposed user credentials** — *Event date: Nov 10–13 intrusion; RIKEN disclosure Nov 16 (JST)*
RIKEN's Center for Computational Science revealed that between Nov 10 and 13 an attacker breached the public web dashboard showing Fugaku's operational status, moved laterally to servers storing registered users' names, emails and authentication information — 4,222 Fugaku users plus 375 on the smaller Rikyu — installed malware on several servers, and on Nov 13 attempted to log into the world's #9 supercomputer itself, prompting RIKEN to pull the 7.6-million-core machine offline while it investigates. RIKEN says there's no evidence yet that research data leaked, but cannot say what did leave; it is coordinating with the organizations whose external services used the same credentials.
**Why it matters:** A read-only status page proved the soft underbelly of a flagship scientific platform — the compromise path was monitoring infrastructure → credential stores → the HPC login front door, a pattern any facility publishing operational dashboards should assume is targetable; the outage also cascades across the Japanese research-scheduling calendar.
**Action:** Fugaku/Rikyu users: rotate every credential that was stored in RIKEN's user database and audit other organizations' accounts reusing them; admins of operational-status dashboards should treat them as authenticated-network neighbors, not display-only assets.
- Source: The Register — "Japan's Fugaku supercomputer has decidedly un-super security," Nov 17, 2026 05:27 UTC — https://www.theregister.com/security/2026-11-17/japans-fugaku-supercomputer-has-decidedly-un-super-security/5310333

## Defensive tools and advisories

**Also noted: Microsoft previews Remote Lock for lost or stolen Windows devices** — *Event date: Nov 16, 2026 (Beta build 26320.10003)*
Microsoft is testing Remote Lock in Windows 11 Insider Beta build 26320.10003 (and Experimental 26340.10013): enterprise IT can lock a lost or stolen Windows device via Intune without wiping it, and only the Intune service can unlock it — a data-preserving middle ground between "do nothing" and full remote wipe for unencrypted or personal-data-heavy laptops.
**Why it matters:** Lock-without-wipe materially changes the loss-playbook for mixed-use devices — the moment an admin can freeze a device while preserving evidence, insider-loss and theft scenarios stop being automatic data-destruction decisions.
