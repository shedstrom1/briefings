## Vulnerabilities and patches

**Microsoft Paint RCE fix (CVE-2026-83844) now available — but only via the Microsoft Store, not Windows Update**
*Event date: Nov 5–6, 2026 (fixed build 11.2609.401.0 newly surfaced; flaw published by Microsoft Oct 13)*
The fix for the out-of-bounds-read RCE in Windows Paint is now shipping in Store build 11.2609.401.0, which The Hacker News confirmed Thursday/Friday as the newest Paint build in all ten countries it checked. Microsoft rates the flaw Important, CVSS 8.1; it is network-exploitable with no sign-in and no user interaction via "a specially crafted packet to an affected service," and there is no workaround or mitigation in the advisory. Critically, the advisory's fixed-build target was quietly changed last week (from 11.2605.91.0 to 11.2609.401.0) with no revision-history entry, while the CVE record still flags only builds below 11.2605.91.0 as affected — so scanners reading the CVE will treat unpatched installs as fixed. Brian Vitko (RFF) found Store offerings still at the unfixed 11.2605.81.0 across six countries earlier this week; Paint is installed per-user plus a provisioned copy, so one account's version check is insufficient. Not in CISA KEV at THN's check.
**Why it matters:** An Important-rated, no-interaction network RCE in a default Windows app that patch scanners actively mis-score, with the delivery channel (Store) decoupled from the channel (Windows Update) most enterprises actually manage, is a silent-exposure scenario — the kind that turns into a KEV entry later.
**Action:** Verify every Paint copy with `Get-AppxPackage -AllUsers -Name Microsoft.Paint` and `Get-AppProvisionedPackage -Online` (PowerShell, as admin) and force ≥11.2609.401.0 via Store auto-update or Intune Store-app scans; don't trust the CVE record's version threshold.
Source: The Hacker News, "Microsoft Paint RCE Fix Now Available, but Windows Update Won't Deliver It," Nov 6, 2026 — https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/microsoft-paint-rce-fix-now-available.html
Source (secondary): Microsoft MSRC / SentinelOne CVE-2026-83844 entry — https://www.sentinelone.com/vulnerability-database/cve-2026-83844/

**Open-source coding agent leaks users' email addresses to every website it visits (Claude Code issue #107757)**
*Event date: Nov 5, 2026 17:13 EST (GitHub issue filed; still open)*
A bug report filed Thursday evening says Anthropic's Claude Code sends the signed-in user's email address in the HTTP `User-Agent` header during agent web requests — exposing it to arbitrary third-party sites. The reporter (v2.1.318, macOS) says it directly fueled scam-email volume and demands removal from the harness, not an opt-out. The issue has drawn 475 👍 reactions and 71 comments and reached the Hacker News front page overnight (1,241 points), with other users confirming the behavior; Anthropic had not publicly responded as of this fire.
**Why it matters:** Agentic CLIs fetch on the user's behalf at machine speed; a PII-bearing User-Agent silently fingerprints every operator and creates a durable phishing/scam-targeting corpus. Expect other agent harnesses to be audited for the same leak.
**Action:** Until fixed, restrict the agent's web tools or egress via proxy with UA rewriting; audit your own agent tooling for identity leakage in headers.
Source: GitHub anthropics/claude-code issue #107757, created Nov 5, 2026 22:13 UTC — https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/107757
Source (community): Hacker News, Nov 5, 2026 22:17 UTC (1,241 points) — https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50385272

## Attacks and campaigns

**US indictment: three charged in $129M AI voice/video CEO-impersonation fraud**
*Event date: Nov 5, 2026 (indictment unsealed Thursday in SDNY; wire coverage Thursday evening–Friday)*
Jacob Soall, 41, Sharon Hazan, 54, and Guy Sheetrit, 42 — operating from Spain, the UK, Israel and the UAE — are charged with wire fraud, aggravated identity theft and money laundering for a July 2024–February 2026 scheme that cloned the voices (and sometimes video likenesses) of corporate CEOs and big-firm attorneys to push accounting departments into wires worldwide, per the SDNY indictment and AP. Victims included hospital systems in Missouri and California, a Rhode Island bank, a UK fintech (>$11.5M in one hit), a Massachusetts mutual-fund company and a Florida pet-food firm; proceeds were converted to cryptocurrency. All three were arrested between June and September and await extradition; each faces up to 20 years per fraud count plus 2 years for identity theft.
**Why it matters:** The largest documented AI-impersonation BEC prosecution to date, and the clearest validation yet of the deepfake-wire-fraud playbook that payment-verification controls (out-of-band callbacks, not "call back the number in the email") still don't fully cover.

**MetaMask post-mortem: staking-rewards intrusion never touched wallets or keys; ~0.385 ETH diverted**
*Event date: Nov 5, 2026 (company's detailed "Thursday" update; BleepingComputer Nov 6 04:22 EST)*
MetaMask's first substantive account of the infrastructure incident it disclosed Sept 30 says a threat actor "gained access to systems used to store recipient addresses and diverted some execution-layer staking rewards" — about 0.385 ETH total — with the issue identified and contained the same day. The company states no evidence customers' staked principal, withdrawal keys, Secret Recovery Phrases or private keys were compromised, that affected systems are secured, and that wallet users need take no action; precautionary validator exits caused temporary withdrawal delays and missed rewards that MetaMask says it will make whole. It declined further technical specifics, redirecting to the public statement.
**Why it matters:** The reassurance is significant for a non-custodial wallet trusted with self-custody keys — but the confirmed compromise of staking-payout plumbing (recipient-address storage) shows execution-layer reward distribution is a soft target that persists for weeks before scope is admitted publicly.

## Defensive tools and advisories

**Update on previously reported (ledger: eu-fridge-wave-2, fridge-wave-danfoss-7country): five US agencies formally warn cold-chain operators to assume targeting**
*Event date: Nov 5, 2026 (joint fact sheet published Thursday; BleepingComputer 19:56 EST)*
CISA, FBI, HHS, USDA and DOT published a joint advisory (IC3 CSA 261105) stating that "malicious cyber actors are targeting vulnerabilities and weaknesses in unpatched or internet-accessible electronic temperature controllers and commercial refrigeration controllers in retail grocery and cold storage facilities" — exploiting authentication bypasses, code-injection flaws and weak configs to force units into continuous heating/defrost cycles, spoil stock, shorten equipment life and create fire hazards while blinding operators' temperature visibility. The advisory lands after the October wave that hit an estimated 250–350 French supermarkets, the November second wave (>115 stores, six countries, some units pushed to 50°C/122°F), and the French Blood Establishment's confirmation that three blood-product cold stores had settings altered. Recommended actions: pull refrigeration controllers off the public internet, secure legitimate remote access, change default passwords, apply vendor updates, isolate OT from business networks, and enforce hardware-side safe temperature limits.
**Why it matters:** This is the escalation from incident-news to doctrine — a multi-agency assumption-of-targeting standard that shifts liability optics onto grocers, cold-storage and healthcare operators running vendor telemetry stacks, and gives OT teams a named mitigation checklist for a device class (ETCs) usually invisible to asset inventories.
**Action:** Audit internet exposure of every electronic temperature controller/refrigeration management system now; the advisory's remediation list is directly executable this week.
