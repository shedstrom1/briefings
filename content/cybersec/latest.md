## Vulnerabilities and patches

### N-able N-central "4EverAble2Shell" zero-day exploited in the wild — 21 servers mine Monero, 19 already on Hotfix 2 (Update on previously reported: 4EverAble2Shell+Agent2Shell, reported this morning 07:44)
**Event date: November 12, 2026.** Researchers confirmed active internet-scale exploitation the same day security researcher Sean Bales published PoCs for 4EverAble2Shell (unauthenticated root RCE when the Legacy API is enabled — chaining Apache Axis **CVE-2026-90436** (CVSS v4 9.8) with an N-central privilege-escalation flaw that abuses the privileged `setLDFileOwner` helper plus a symlink to make a root-owned script writable, then executes via `serverControl`) and Agent2Shell (claimed SYSTEM on Windows agents via 10+ zero-days). Both were tested against **N-central Appliance 2026.4.1.26 HF2**, the November 10 emergency hotfix — i.e. "patched" is not enough. Previdian saw probing of its honeypot from **157.20.241[.]16** at ~08:30 UTC and full exploit-chain attempts; Huntback counted **21 compromised production servers** at 12:50 UTC (all submitted mining shares within five minutes; workers named `nc_<server-ip>`), running XMRig at ~50% of cores across the US, Canada, South Africa, UK, Germany, France and Sweden — 19 on the latest HF2. N-able's November 12 advisory says it is "restricting certain N-central features and integrations," has disabled the Intune integration and applied protections to hosted environments.
**Why it matters:** an RMM root compromise is a pivot into every MSP-managed customer estate; cryptojacking today is a foothold for supply-chain raids tomorrow — the same pattern as the SCKit/ScreenConnect MSP campaign reported this morning.
**Severity:** no CVE assigned to 4EverAble2Shell itself; chained Apache Axis CVE-2026-90436 is 9.8 (NVD). Previdian tracks the N-central LPE as VULN-2026-0009 in its own KEV.
**Action (per sources):** disable Legacy API (default-off — verify), hunt `/dms/services/ServerUI` requests referencing `jline.internal.TerminalLineSettings`, unexpected `xvd.service` units and files under `/tmp/.xvd` or `/var/tmp/.xvd`; treat any hit as full root compromise; keep agents/probes on HF2 and expect further hotfixes.

## Attacks and campaigns

### DOJ/FBI seize $1.2B from the Binance heist and disrupt North Korea's "FaceStealer" video-call scheme
**Event date: November 12, 2026 (DOJ press release 26-1356, "Thursday, November 12"; afternoon FBI press call).** The Justice Department obtained court authorization to seize or restrain **more than $1.2 billion** of the ~$6.3 billion stolen from Binance on October 29 by the North Korean military-linked **TraderTraitor** cluster — officials stressed it is part of the ~$2.7 billion already frozen, not additional recovery, and that DOJ now has custody pending forfeiture proceedings. Separately (and likely by different DPRK actors), the FBI disrupted cloud infrastructure behind **FaceStealer**, the video-conferencing social-engineering operation that has netted **>$400 million in 18 months** (groups tracked as Sapphire Sleet / UNC1069 / Dangerous Password / CryptoCore / APT38 / BlueNoroff / Stardust Chollima): Calendly invites from previously compromised accounts lure targets to lookalike Zoom/Teams portals, pre-recorded videos of trusted contacts "join" the meeting, a fake audio glitch delivers a "fix" that installs a cookie harvester, keylogger, screen-capturer and backdoor — and the session secretly records the new victim's webcam for the next attack. Investigators seized **900+ webcam recordings**. Notably, FaceStealer has expanded from crypto/Web3 into the **open-source supply chain**: an FBI official said a developer maintaining "widely used software" was compromised and used to plant malicious code that was stopped before wide spread, and this spring a maintainer of a significant JavaScript library nearly fell for a fake Teams invite (the scheme traces to the APT38 cluster that hit debug/chalk and axios).
**Why it matters:** the Binance freeze returns roughly half the largest crypto theft on record to legal custody, while FaceStealer's playbook — social trust, not software bugs — is exactly what no EDR cleanly stops, and its OSS-maintainer targeting turns a single phony meeting into a downstream code-execution vector for thousands of dependents.
**Severity:** Not specified by source (law-enforcement action, no CVSS).
**Action (per sources):** FBI sounds the alarm on video-call "audio-fix" instructions, treats unverified meeting fixes as credential/crypto kill-chain, and urges VASPs/OSS projects to harden maintainer contact channels.

### South Korea's Financial Security Institute apologizes for portal hack; FSC targeted audit starts November 16
**Event date: November 12, 2026 (apology and audit plan; the underlying intrusion was detected November 9 and first reported November 10 — not previously covered by this briefing).** Park Sang-won, head of the Financial Security Institute — the FSC-led "gatekeeper" cyber agency for Korean finance since 2015 — publicly apologized at FISCON 2026 in Yeouido after attackers accessed the institute's continuous information-protection assessment site where firms submit security-evaluation documents: an estimated **130 files from 48 financial companies** (many mutual-finance cooperatives) have leaked so far, with the FSC/Financial Supervisory Service on-site inspection escalating to a **targeted audit from November 16**. Separately, from November 23 the FSS will block legacy encryption on its FINES/EDES reporting links (TLS 1.2+ mandatory). Institute researchers also used the conference to warn AI-accelerated "machine-speed" attacks demand automated detection.
**Why it matters:** the breach hits the assessor itself — the submitted files are effectively blueprints of each firm's security posture, so downstream exposure compounds beyond the 48 named institutions.
**Severity:** Not specified by source.

## Defensive tools and advisories

### Anthropic releases self-hosted Claude Managed Agents — harness, sandboxes, credentials and session history stay in customer infrastructure
**Event date: November 12, 2026 (Anthropic product announcement; announcement time of day not verified by source).** Customers can run the full Claude Managed Agents service — agent harness, tool-execution sandboxes, credentials and session history — in their own cloud account or on-prem data center, choosing where requests to the model go (Anthropic or a chosen provider); access is via the Anthropic account team. Help Net Security frames it as keeping agent credentials inside the customer's environment — a direct answer to the agent-credential incidents covered this week (stolen Claude API keys turned into memory-store C2, Fireworks/Notion exposure).
**Why it matters:** for regulated and OT environments this removes the biggest blocker to agentic automation (third-party custody of secrets/session data) — and shifts the hard problem to sandbox integrity and egress policy on the customer's side.
**Severity:** n/a (product release).
