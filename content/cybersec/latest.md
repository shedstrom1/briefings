## Vulnerabilities and patches

**F5 ships emergency out-of-band fixes for five critical BIG-IP APM/iRules stack overflows** — advisory/hotfixes Wednesday–Friday; CVEs published with Friday's coverage.
F5 released out-of-band hotfixes for five critical stack-based buffer-overflow flaws — CVE-2026-121805, CVE-2026-120292, CVE-2026-120285, CVE-2026-116953 and CVE-2026-123637 — in the BIG-IP Access Policy Manager centralized access-management proxy and iRules scripting feature. Unauthenticated attackers can crash the Traffic Management Microkernel and, on vulnerable configurations, achieve remote code execution; F5 rates all five Critical and says there are no mitigation measures, only the fixes. A sixth issue, CVE-2026-126822 (integer underflow in APM SAML handling, High, DoS), also got an engineering hotfix. This extends the six-flaw disclosure reported Wednesday (CVSS 9.8/v4 9.3 for that set). No exploitation reported yet, but Shadowserver tracks more than 177,000 internet-facing BIG-IP instances and 14,600+ with APM fingerprints; CISA has flagged seven F5 flaws as exploited since 2021.
Why it matters: BIG-IP sits in front of enterprise apps worldwide, and F5 boxes have repeatedly been nation-state and ransomware staging targets — an unpatched data-plane box can be both spied through and used to pivot.
Action: install the out-of-band hotfixes or upgrade as soon as possible; treat APM-facing virtual servers with unusual iRules/APM agent configs as highest priority.

**WatchGuard fixes 15 Dimension flaws, including an unauthenticated RCE in the log collector** — advisory dated Nov 19; reported Nov 20.
WatchGuard Dimension 2.0 through pre-2.3.2 carries 15 CVEs fixed in version 2.3.2, headlined by CVE-2026-86130, an improper-authentication flaw (reported 8.7) in the log-ingestion service that allows code execution with no valid credentials or admin session. Three other findings score 9.3, and the advisory includes root-level code execution (CVE-2026-93914, CVE-2026-103580), SQL injection in the collector (CVE-2026-93911, 8.7), session-token exposures, CSRF to create a Super Administrator (CVE-2026-86126) and persistent non-expiring sessions (CVE-2026-93916). No exploitation reported.
Why it matters: log collectors are deliberately reachable from many networks to receive telemetry even when the web console is firewalled — that broad ingress is exactly the surface CVE-2026-86130 removes credentials from.
Action: upgrade Dimension to 2.3.2+; until then limit collector access to known log-source networks and restrict the management interface, per WatchGuard guidance; then verify the version and that log sources are still reporting.

## Attacks and campaigns

**Bitrise post-mortem: ARTEX AI agents entered through a dormant no-2FA bot account; the platform was shut down to break the chain** — vendor report published Nov 19 (first request Oct 25, decisive breach Nov 13).
Bitrise detailed a 25-day ARTEX operation: three days of public-surface mapping under user agents claiming to be an "authorized pentest" (Oct 25), a return Nov 12 as a normal customer running test builds, then a Nov 13 11:46:48 UTC sign-in to a dormant internal test-bot account with no 2FA. Within 11 minutes the agents were calling cloud APIs with the workspace's service keys and chaining credentials for ten hours into signing files, source code, infrastructure state and build logs. Detection came at 16:32 UTC, a platform-wide shutdown began 19:26 and Bitrise was dark by 20:34; the last successful cloud call logged at 21:57. Cleanup was scorching: all 919 cloud service-account keys revoked, every production secret rotated, build infrastructure rebuilt from trusted sources across three data centers. GCP audit logs show 8,725 attacker entries (5,662 successful) across 72 tried identities; when the attacker replayed stolen material Nov 18, all 19 requests were refused. Files in 15 customer workspaces were accessed — one code-signing certificate (password unreachable), one provisioning profile, and 13 diagnostic build logs from May.
Why it matters: this is the most granular public anatomy yet of an ARTEX intrusion — and the entry point was hygiene, not a zero-day. A single stale bot account without MFA turned weeks of stealthy recon into a ten-hour credential chain that forced a full platform outage.
Action: audit dormant accounts (especially bot/service identities) for missing 2FA and workspace scope; alert on a single source cycling credential header types against a control-plane API and repeatedly failing; never log credential values.

**European Commission confirms AI-assisted cyberattack, says no evidence of a data breach** — official confirmation Thursday briefing, reported Nov 19–20.
Spokesperson Thomas Regnier confirmed the Commission suffered an AI-assisted cyberattack detected October 22 and confirmed October 26, now largely contained after the Commission temporarily took applications offline — including the Sysper HR platform, the MyPMO medical-reimbursement system and translation services. The investigation found no evidence of a data breach of staff information; Regnier would not attribute the attack or name the AI tool, and said the Commission received no ransom demand. The disclosure lands roughly two weeks after CERT-EU warned that the open-source ARTEX framework's public availability "will likely accelerate adoption by threat actors" — ARTEX has been tied to Barracuda, Railway and ConnectWise intrusions, and Politico (first to report) says staff data may potentially have been compromised over the 10-day-plus disruption.
Why it matters: a supranational government openly confirming an AI-tool-assisted intrusion — without attribution or ransom — normalizes agentic attacks as disruption-first operations, and the gap between Politico's scale reporting and the Commission's "no evidence of breach" line remains unresolved.
Action: none stated; treat unattributed AI-tool intrusions with long dwell as a distinct class in threat modeling.

**N-able reclassifies email-security cloud outage as a cyberattack; credentials may have been stolen** — vendor statement Nov 18–20.
N-able told SecurityWeek the days-long Mail Assure and SpamExperts Hosted Cloud disruption that began November 16 was caused by attackers compromising a single web server, where "an unauthorized party may have been able to access certain credentials stored on the platform" — a hard reversal from its initial "platform configuration and service dependencies" explanation. Mail filtering still runs, but the management portal and APIs stay disabled across the Americas, APAC and Europe; N-able has rotated internal platform credentials, revoked existing customer API tokens, invalidated OAuth tokens and reset local user passwords, and says the incident is unrelated to the N-central zero-day episode. No customer-data compromise confirmed.
Why it matters: email-security management planes hold senders, policies and quarantine data; if stored credentials leaked unrotated, spoofing and quarantine-blindness windows could follow — and customers using API tokens must expect rotation.
Action: rotate any Mail Assure/SpamExperts API tokens and admin passwords on your side, follow N-able's per-customer reconfiguration guidance, and watch for spoofed domain mail while the portal is down.

**Hunt.io: Russian-speaking credential-theft operation ran 603 implants, loot store found wide open on a Dutch VPS** — research reported Nov 20.
Hunt.io found an unauthenticated open directory on a Netherlands VPS holding the live loot store of an infostealer operation: decrypted password-manager vaults, cleartext bank logins, and credentials behind federal identity gateways ID.me and Login.gov. The operators ran three custom tools — HavoX (C2), Phantom53 (victim/task management) and Kraken Bridge (external control panel) — and the 603 infected Windows PCs generated over 1.2 million connections to three C2 systems since October 12. At least 57 victims had US bank or government logins on their machines; 46 had federal benefits or identity-service accounts. In one case an entire LastPass vault was cracked offline, yielding 2,880 logins including a bank account, the IRS and the vault's sharing key. Chrome's app-bound encryption was bypassed using hardcoded AES-GCM keys for Chrome versions 20 and 21, and a script triaged stolen cookies for 28 high-value services. No bank or agency was breached — the theft happened on victims' endpoints.
Why it matters: this is account-takeover fuel assembled from ordinary users' PCs, and it explicitly includes the identity brokers many US services treat as a second factor.
Action: Hunt.io recommends phishing-resistant authentication, session and device binding, and fast customer resets — for your users, that means hardware passkeys and no password reuse across banking and federal services.

**ROMULUS: Blackpoint frames a ClickFix platform that delivers the new CNCMachineRMS RAT over DNS-over-HTTPS** — research coverage Nov 20.
Blackpoint Cyber's APG unit describes ROMULUS not as one malware family but as a delivery service using ClickFix lures and signed-binary DLL sideloading to distribute at least two implants, including the previously undocumented CNCMachineRMS — a RAT with remote desktop control, screen streaming, microphone and camera capture, file management, system reconnaissance and on-demand payload execution, talking over DNS over HTTPS. In one chain ROMULUS pushed an MSI posing as "Stardock Smart DeElevation Tool" that sideloaded a malicious "I++u.dll" to decode shellcode; the same implant also arrived via ChatGPT Custom GPT ClickFix lures and through BabaYada Loader abusing a legitimately signed IBM SPSS executable. Coverage also links the ecosystem to broader ClickFix industrialization: fake Cloudflare CAPTCHAs on 70,000+ compromised sites, fake Zillow review pages installing NetSupport, and a WordPress plugin-injection chain named TRUST#GRAFT.
Why it matters: RAT-as-a-service with DoH C2 and borrowed legitimate installers keeps slipping past signature-based detection, and ClickFix is now the delivery wrapper for most of it.
Action: block/monitor msiexec and PowerShell launches of installers from browser download paths, alert on DoH-heavy traffic from workstations, and kill ClickFix-class copy-paste-command flows with browser-policy controls.

**USDA "security incident" knocks farmer payment and loan tools offline; databases and passwords reset across environments** — internal-email reports Nov 19–20.
An internal email from USDA's acting assistant CIO for the Farm Production and Conservation Business Center, Chris Rottler, told departments the department's Cybersecurity and Privacy Operations Center "uncovered a security incident" that forced resets of all database passwords in all environments and is causing application outages — affecting portals used for crop-insurance and other producer payments and loans, which one trade outlet says have been flaky since Monday. USDA has not publicly confirmed the incident or any data theft; the agency's website briefly went "down for maintenance" Thursday evening and staff reported VPN problems.
Why it matters: farm-program payment systems are a proven ransomware and espionage target (the FSA 2024 shutdown is still remembered), and an unexplained forced global password reset suggests more than routine maintenance — while the absence of any public USDA statement extends the current week's outage-credibility gap.
Action: growers and lenders should expect payment/loan portal slowness, avoid acting on lookalike "reset required" notices until USDA confirms, and report anomalies via the FPAC helpdesk.

## Defensive tools and advisories

**SonicWall orders immediate retirement of all Gen 6 firewalls: they will never get security patches again** — vendor notice reported Nov 20.
SonicWall urged every customer still running Generation 6 (SonicOS 6.x) firewalls to retire and replace them immediately and stop exposing them at the internet edge, saying it "expects internet-facing Gen 6 firewalls to be actively targeted" as attackers use automation and AI to find and exploit unsupported edge devices faster than ever. The affected model list spans the TZ350/400/500/600 lines, SOHO, NSA/NSa 2650–9650, SuperMassive 9200–9800, NSsp 12400/12800 and NSv virtual appliances — devices that will not receive fixes even for critical flaws. SonicWall told customers to treat any Gen 6 firewall still in service as high-risk exposure and migrate to Gen 8 hardware running SonicOS 8.
Why it matters: end-of-life edge boxes have anchored multiple mass campaigns this year, and SonicWall is publishing the rarest thing from a vendor — an explicit "expect targeting" warning for its own unsupported installed base.
Action: inventory SonicOS 6.x devices now, cut internet exposure today (not after the replacement arrives), and schedule Gen 8/SonicOS 8 migration; treat firewall EOL status as an incident-driver risk in the register.

**UK ran a secret nationwide operation to pull tens of thousands of fridges off the internet** — BBC disclosure Nov 20.
The BBC revealed a months-long covert campaign — first withheld to avoid handing attackers a roadmap — in which researchers, IT teams and the NCSC, Defra and the Food Standards Agency secured tens of thousands of refrigeration devices at UK supermarkets, warehouses, restaurants, petrol stations and stadiums. The trigger: in late October, Omega Solutions found and Pen Test Partners verified the same weak-password disease in UK-market Resource Data Management (RDM) controllers that drove the Danfoss-based October–November attacks on the continent; teams took thousands of controllers off the open internet remotely or by physically yanking cables while monitoring temperatures by hand. RDM's CEO disputes the critical comparison (hardware required, he said) but has shipped initial patches, praised the disclosure process, and confirmed no malicious activity observed.
Why it matters: the European fridge waves spoiled tonnes of food and hit blood banks; the UK's near-miss shows cold-chain controllers are now standard adversary terrain, and that secret mitigation campaigns are becoming a real tool of national cyber defense.
Action: cold-chain operators everywhere should audit controller default credentials, pull management interfaces off the internet, and adopt manual temperature fallbacks.

**White House fires national cyber director Sean Cairncross after the week of outages** — reported Nov 20.
The administration dismissed its national cyber director, a person close to it told Reuters — the first major personnel move since the Microsoft 365 outage, the FAA nationwide ground stop and the NSANet disruption, none of which officials say is confirmed as a cyberattack. DoD CIO Kirsten Davies takes the role temporarily; the eventual successor needs Senate confirmation, per Reuters. Cairncross called serving "the honor of a lifetime." Senator Josh Hawley this week pressed for a briefing to Congress on the outages.
Why it matters: US cyber policy leadership just went into caretaker mode during an unexplained multi-system outage streak — with midterms months away and CISA's own leadership also in flux (LoSapio nomination unconfirmed), coordination risk rises even if no link to any outage is proven.
Action: federal-facing teams should watch for guidance churn on incident reporting and BOD deadlines during the interim.
