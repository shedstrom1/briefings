## Vulnerabilities and patches

**Microsoft ships OOB Windows updates to distrust eMudhra certificates** — *Update on previously reported (ledger: "eMudhra/emSign 8 intermediate CA keys compromised; browsers + Go/NSS/certifi emergency distrust Oct 20")*
Event date: updates released October 26; Microsoft confirmed the change in a statement covered by BleepingComputer today at 2:20 PM ET. The out-of-band patches KB5134133 (Windows 11 24H2/25H2/26H2) and KB5134125 (Windows 10 21H2/22H2) strip seven certificates — six eMudhra G3 roots and an AAA Certificate Services cross-sign — from the Windows trust list, targeting systems that can't receive automatic Certificate Trust List updates. Microsoft separately removes the same seven certificates from the Edge trust list today and says it is still investigating the remaining eMudhra roots. Microsoft is explicit that this is a broader trust decision than the earlier Disallowed CTL blocks on the eight compromised intermediates: roots used to chain-verify other eMudhra-issued certificates are now gone.
Severity: Not specified by source (trust-list change, not a CVE).
**Why it matters:** Root removal breaks any certificate still chaining to the deleted roots, so compatibility surprises hit offline and air-gapped environments hardest — precisely the systems these OOB packages exist for. The "investigating remaining roots" language signals more distrust waves are likely.
**Action:** Install KB5134133/KB5134125 on disconnected or CTL-disabled Windows systems, and inventory certificates that depend on the removed roots before they fail; locally re-trust only after your own security assessment (Microsoft's guidance).
Source: "Microsoft releases Windows OOB updates to distrust eMudhra certificates," BleepingComputer, Lawrence Abrams, October 27, 2026 2:20 PM EDT — https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/microsoft/microsoft-releases-windows-oob-updates-to-distrust-emudhra-certificates/

## Attacks and campaigns

**Brazilian Magecart gang skims card data from every Convertr checkout via hijacked Plausible analytics server**
Event dates: skimmer planted October 24; Sansec published its analysis October 26 (initial disclosure — first coverage by this briefing); Convertr removed the skimmer and Sansec confirmed the tracker clean today (12:54–13:08 UTC); BleepingComputer's independent report published today at 4:57 PM ET. Convertr — the Brazilian e-commerce platform powering online stores and checkouts for dozens of major fashion brands, with more than R$2 billion (~$370M) in merchant sales — self-hosted an unpatched Plausible Community Edition (3.0–3.2.0). An attacker appended an obfuscated eval'd skimmer to the one tracker file every Convertr checkout loads, harvesting card number, expiry, CVV, cardholder name and CPF (Brazil's tax ID) and POSTing base64 records to `https://1x0.is/ws`. Sansec suspects — but could not confirm — entry via CVE-2026-8467, the unauthenticated `/storybook` command-execution flaw Plausible fixed in May 2026. The group, tracked since July 2024, refined the skimmer within two days to target Convertr's exact payment-API fields; this is its first supply-chain-scale attack.
Severity: Not specified by source (suspected entry CVE-2026-8467 rated critical by Plausible's own advisory; fixed in CE 3.2.1).
**Why it matters:** One file on an analytics server equated to every checkout across dozens of brands — the classic Magecart lesson, re-proven on the vendor side. Sansec's timeline shows the platform still served the skimmer on October 26 after notification.
**Action:** Consumers who paid by card at a Brazilian store whose checkout URL contained `checkout.convertr` since October 24 should block/replace the card and watch CPF-linked fraud; browsers can cache the infected script up to 24 hours after cleanup. Plausible CE self-hosters: upgrade to ≥3.2.1, hunt `/storybook` requests in access logs, and rebuild + rotate secrets if found. Convertr says it is notifying merchants, payment providers and authorities.
Source: "Hackers hijack Convertr analytics script to steal payment card data," BleepingComputer, Bill Toulas, October 27, 2026 4:57 PM EDT — https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hackers-hijack-convertr-analytics-script-to-steal-payment-card-data/
Second source: "Notorious Brazilian Magecart group hijacks Convertr checkouts via Plausible analytics," Sansec Forensics Team, October 26, 2026 (updated October 27 with removal confirmation, IOCs and timeline) — https://sansec.io/research/convertr-plausible-supply-chain-attack

**Nefilim/Hive ransomware affiliate sentenced to 70 months**
Event date: sentencing today, October 27 (DOJ press release 26-1265). Artem Stryzhak, 36, a Ukrainian national living in Barcelona, was sentenced in federal court in Brooklyn to 70 months for conspiracy to commit computer fraud. He got Nefilim ransomware code access in June 2021 for a 20% cut of proceeds, ran attacks through his "panel" account, and helped research high-revenue US/Canada/Australia targets; DOJ puts the group's victim count at roughly 1,500 worldwide — hospitals, government entities, law firms — with over $100 million in losses. Arrested in Spain June 2024, extradited April 30; co-conspirator Volodymyr Tymoshchuk remains at large with an $11 million State Department Transnational Organized Crime reward open.
Severity: Not applicable (sentencing).
**Why it matters:** A rare high-profile Nefilim/Hive conviction closes a multi-continent case (FBI Springfield led; EDNY + ND Fla prosecuting) and keeps pressure on the Tymoshchuk manhunt; DOJ framed it as proof ransomware operators can't hide abroad.
Source: "Ukrainian National Sentenced for Role in Hacking Hospitals, Businesses, and Governments as Part of Ransomware Conspiracies Causing Over $100 Million in Losses," DOJ Office of Public Affairs, October 27, 2026 — https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/ukrainian-national-sentenced-role-hacking-hospitals-businesses-and-governments-part (The Record also reported today: "Nefilim, Hive ransomware operator given five-year-plus sentence after guilty plea.")

**DOJ indicts six in Madagascar fake-NFT scam ring after FBI sting seized 3,000+ devices**
Event date: indictments announced Tuesday October 27 (midday ET). The DOJ Scam Center Strike Force charged Malaysian national Chui Kok Wa and five others (from Cameroon, Rwanda, Kenya, Uganda) over a romance-to-crypto "pig butchering" scheme run from compounds in Antananarivo: ~20 workers lured US victims via dating sites into fake NFT investments, siphoning $516,695+ from 74 crypto wallets across Maryland, Oregon, Nevada, Minnesota and beyond; the operation moved to Madagascar after Cambodian takedowns. An August FBI trip with Madagascar National Police hit 13 compounds and accessed 3,000+ devices — but DOJ says it was "stymied": suspects were repatriated to China before interviews (at least 49 Chinese nationals extradited Monday), and a material-witness warrant targets 15 employees who claim trafficking while device evidence shows paid employment contracts. Chainalysis and Tether assisted tracing; the $8.65B estimate for US scam-compound losses in 2025 anchored DOJ's framing.
Severity: Not applicable (law-enforcement action).
**Why it matters:** Concrete evidence the scam-center industry is migrating from Southeast Asia to Africa — and a rare look inside its economics ($900/month plus commission, US-hours shifts, scripted catfishing).
Source: "US indicts Malaysian national after Madagascar scam operation uncovered," The Record (Jonathan Greig), October 27, 2026 — https://therecord.media/malaysian-national-indicted-after-scam-centers-raid-madagascar ; DOJ D.C. U.S. Attorney's Office announcement (same day), Chainalysis confirmed its DOJ assistance on X.
