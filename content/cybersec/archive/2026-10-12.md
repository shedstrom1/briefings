## Vulnerabilities and patches

### GitLab warns of maximum-severity RCE in CI/CD Regular Expression Compiler — patch immediately
**Event date:** October 12, 2026 (GitLab advisory and warning)

GitLab told customers to immediately patch a maximum-severity vulnerability in the CI/CD Regular Expression Compiler that could let remote attackers run arbitrary code on targeted instances. BleepingComputer reported the warning Monday morning, noting GitLab published the advisory and urged patching "today" rather than rolling it into a scheduled security release cycle. GitLab has had a rough stretch on this front — a different max-severity GitLab flaw (CVE-2026-85706) was added to CISA's KEV in mid-September and drew probing within a day — so expect fast exploitation interest here. The specific CVE identifier was not available in the primary sources read during this run (GitLab's public advisory database did not yet surface the core-product entry at collection time).

**Severity (as published):** "Maximum severity" per GitLab advisory as reported by BleepingComputer; numeric CVSS not specified in the fetched source.

**Why it matters:** CI/CD platforms hold source code, deployment secrets and signing keys; a pre-text RCE in the pipeline machinery is a supply-chain-scale breach risk for every org with an exposed self-managed instance, and the exploit window between disclosure and mass scanning is now measured in hours.

**Action:** Apply GitLab's advisory immediately — upgrade to the fixed versions GitLab lists; prioritize internet-facing self-managed instances.

Source: "GitLab warns of max severity code execution vulnerability," BleepingComputer (Sergiu Gatlan), October 12, 2026 09:09 AM ET — https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/feed/ (article indexed in BleepingComputer's security feed; see also BleepingComputer's GitLab tag page https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/tag/gitlab/)

### SonicWall SMA1000 CVSS 10.0 flaw (CVE-2026-102255) hit with 135 exploitation attempts within days of patch
**Event date:** exploitation first observed October 8, 2026; attempt counts published October 12, 2026 (flaw disclosed October 6)

Threat actors are exploiting CVE-2026-102255 (CVSS score: 10.0), a pre-authenticated server-side request forgery in the SMA 1000 Work Place interface, within days of SonicWall's October 6 disclosure. Defused saw the first attempts reach its SMA decoys on October 8; Previdian (formerly KEVIntel) has now logged 135 exploitation attempts, reportedly from just two US-based IP addresses, and Horizon3.ai warns successful exploitation can mean full device takeover. SonicWall has shipped fixes in 12.4.3-03670 and 12.5.0-03082 for the affected 6210, 7210 and 8200v models, but had not updated its advisory to reflect the in-the-wild activity as of the reporting.

**Severity (as published):** CVSS score 10.0 (SonicWall/vendor rating as cited by SecurityWeek and The Hacker News).

**Why it matters:** 135 attempts from two IPs looks like someone building a target inventory rather than opportunistic spraying — SSRF on a remote-access appliance pivots straight into internal networks, and SMA1000 boxes have a long history of post-disclosure race conditions.

**Action:** Patch to 12.5.0-03082 or 12.4.3-03670 now; if you can't, isolate or front SMA1000 management/Work Place interfaces and hunt for anomalous internal requests originated by the appliance.

## Attacks and campaigns

### "Unprecedented" cyberattack defrosts refrigerators across French supermarket chains
**Event date:** overnight Sunday October 11–Monday October 12, 2026 (confirmed by victims and prosecutor Monday)

Attackers hijacked refrigeration controllers running older Danfoss control software and triggered their built-in automatic defrost cycle, driving fridge and freezer temperatures up to 50°C (120°F) — Intermarché reported six hours at 50°C and dumped all affected stock. Reported victims span Intermarché, Coopérative U (100+ stores), E.Leclerc, Carrefour Market, Monoprix and Super U; French media count 250–350+ affected stores, with two Alsace supermarkets alone reporting €270,000 in losses and national damage estimated in the millions of euros. Coopérative U president Dominique Schelcher called the attack "extremely serious… unprecedented," EU food commissioner Christophe Hansen condemned the "weaponisation of food," and the Paris prosecutor's office confirmed Monday it is investigating the "large-scale cyberattack" including reported food losses and the outbreak of fires. The intrusion path into the Danfoss controllers is unknown; CISA warned in May 2025 of a high-severity authentication bypass (CVE-2025-41450) in the AK-SM 8xxA platform, with no evidence yet of a link.

**Severity (as published):** Not specified by source (physical-impact incident; no CVSS).

**Why it matters:** This is a rare coordinated IT→OT attack on food retail at national scale — deliberately spoiling food supply is sabotage with economic and public-health teeth, and it shows attackers walking into store-equipment management paths that most retail security programs never treat as attack surface.

**Action:** Retail/food-sector organizations should inventory refrigeration and other facility controllers (Danfoss AK-SM and similar), verify firmware versions, pull OT/BMS/equipment-management interfaces off the public internet, and segment them from corporate networks.

### Ahold Delhaize takes systems offline after confirming cybersecurity incident
**Event date:** October 12, 2026 (company press release, 08:29 Amsterdam; incident running since Sunday)

Ahold Delhaize — roughly 9,400 stores and $104 billion in annual sales — published a terse inside-information release Monday confirming "a security issue within its network" and that it had "taken some systems offline to help protect them." Albert Heijn, Etos and Gall & Gall websites, apps and loyalty programs were down in the Benelux, Delhaize reported back-end trouble in Belgium, US staff at Stop & Shop couldn't reach schedules and pay stubs, and a Food Lion internal memo told stores they "may be unable to shop, stage, or hand off orders." No attack vector, data-theft status or ransomware claim has been confirmed; the company's stores remain open. This is the group's second major incident since a November 2024 ransomware attack (claimed by INC Ransom) exposed data of more than 2.2 million individuals. Whether it relates to the same weekend's French refrigeration attack hitting different chains is not established.

**Severity (as published):** Not specified by source.

**Why it matters:** A top-10 global retailer preemptively cutting its own systems offline signals a containment-first read of an active intrusion — and the ripple effects (order handoff, payroll access, loyalty/payment rails) show how quickly a retail network compromise becomes a customer-facing outage.

**Action:** If you run retail or share supply chains with the group's brands, monitor for order-fulfillment and authentication degradation, and treat vendor status pages (newsroom.aholddelhaize.com) as the authoritative channel rather than social-media rumors.

### Ledger confirms hidden hardware implant in CryptoBilis-sold wallets amid $86–93M crypto theft
**Event date:** drains reported October 9, 2026; Ledger's confirmation update October 11, 2026

Ledger confirmed that one of the devices drained in a wave of Southeast Asian fund losses contained an unauthorized hardware implant, and that all confirmed cases involve wallets sold through reseller CryptoBilis; on-chain researchers put suspected losses between $86 million (Specter) and $93 million (Bitquery tracing), with roughly $70 million in USDT on Tron. Ex-Mt. Gox CEO Mark Karpelès, who says he bought a tampered device in Malaysia, described a micro-controller board with antenna, LTE chip and e-SIM hidden under the display that captures the seed phrase as it's shown and transmits it — devices that pass Ledger's Genuine Check and arrive in intact shrink wrap. Ledger's October 11 update said its own systems and direct sales channels were not compromised; CryptoBilis has halted all hardware-wallet sales. An unverified claim that Mediamarkt-sold European devices were similarly tampered with has not been addressed by Ledger.

**Severity (as published):** Not specified by source (financial losses per researchers, above).

**Why it matters:** A physical seed-phrase exfiltration implant that survives Genuine Check breaks the core threat model of cold storage — and it proves hardware supply-chain tampering is now a mass-scale, monetized operation, not a one-off intercept story.

**Action:** Buyers: never initialize a CryptoBilis-purchased Ledger (last 90 days of purchases are Ledger's stated scope, though 80%+ of drained wallets first received funds in June or later); if set up, move assets to a new device bought from Ledger directly, with a new seed. Treat any "fund recovery" outreach as a scam.

### Ransomware operations Qilin and Settra exploit PAN-OS GlobalProtect auth bypass (CVE-2026-0257) for stealthy VPN entry
**Event date:** ReliaQuest threat update confirming Settra's participation published October 11, 2026 (vulnerability disclosed May 13, 2026; in KEV since May 29)

ReliaQuest reported Monday that the Settra ransomware operation has joined Qilin in actively exploiting CVE-2026-0257, an authentication bypass in PAN-OS GlobalProtect portal/gateway and Prisma Access that lets credential-less attackers establish unauthorized but "authorized-looking" VPN sessions when authentication-override cookies are enabled with a particular certificate configuration. Because intruders ride the org's own VPN, their traffic resembles normal remote work while they run Cobalt Strike, BloodHound/SharpHound, steal credentials and stage encryption — Arctic Wolf Labs previously traced several intrusions from this vector straight to domain-wide Qilin encryption. ReliaQuest assesses exploitation is highly likely to continue for the next three months.

**Severity (as published):** CVSS 7.8 (High), Palo Alto Networks advisory, with its highest suggested response urgency.

**Why it matters:** The same remote-access cookie trust relationship is now an initial-access marketplace feeding at least two ransomware brands — perimeter patching no longer retires the risk, because pre-patch implant sessions survive fixes.

**Action:** Upgrade PAN-OS/Prisma Access per the vendor fixed-version table, disable authentication override cookies where unnecessary, generate a dedicated certificate for override cookies, then terminate all active GlobalProtect sessions and hunt unexpected connections (ReliaQuest flags odd hostnames such as "kali") after patching.

Sources:

- Vendor advisory: "CVE-2026-0257 PAN-OS: GlobalProtect Authentication Bypass Vulnerabilities," Palo Alto Networks — https://security.paloaltonetworks.com/CVE-2026-0257

## Defensive tools and advisories

### Microsoft: Windows 11 23H2 Enterprise/Education/IoT falls out of support in 30 days
**Event date:** October 12, 2026 (Microsoft reminder; EOL November 10, 2026)

Microsoft reminded customers that Windows 11 23H2 Enterprise, Education and IoT Enterprise editions stop receiving security updates next month — a quiet end-of-support that lands two days before the October Patch Tuesday. This stacks on Microsoft's separate warning that devices that never took the upcoming Windows Update certificate replacements will lose Windows Update entirely when current certificates expire in May–June 2027.

**Severity (as published):** Not specified by source (lifecycle notice).

**Why it matters:** Fleet owners routinely forget non-consumer SKU EOLs; 30-day grace means unsupported 23H2 boxes will be sitting on unpatched CVEs from November 10 onward.

**Action:** Move 23H2 Enterprise/Education/IoT devices to a supported release (or enroll in ESU) before November 10, and stage the Windows Update certificate rotation on older devices now.

Source: "Microsoft: Windows 11 23H2 reaches end of support in 30 days," BleepingComputer, October 12, 2026 07:58 AM ET (via https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/feed/)
