## Vulnerabilities and patches

### Apple out-of-cycle updates fix actively exploited Brotli bug plus five Bluetooth/AirPlay pairing flaws
**Event date:** releases shipped Monday, November 9; detail confirmed by SecurityWeek on November 11, 2026 (2:00 AM ET). *Update on previously reported:* `apple-ios-2721-2673-imessage` (footnoted 2026-11-10). Apple patched 10 vulnerabilities across nine builds — iOS/iPadOS 27.2.1, 26.7.3 and 18.7.12; macOS Golden Gate 27.2.1, Tahoe 26.7.3 and Sequoia 15.8.3; watchOS 27.2.1/26.6.1; visionOS 27.2.1. Half are Bluetooth/AirPlay pairing weaknesses: CVE-2026-113968 (AirPlay pairing-code rate-limit bypass from Bluetooth range), CVE-2026-113969 (same bypass via rapportd on the same network), CVE-2026-113964 (CoreUtils — pair without user interaction), CVE-2026-113965 (malicious Bluetooth device pairs without interaction) and CVE-2026-113970 (a Bluetooth use-after-free). The releases also close two iMessage group-chat flaws (CVE-2026-113961, CVE-2026-113960) — and, newly confirmed, CVE-2026-111497, the Brotli out-of-bounds write dubbed Node2Shell that has been exploited in the wild and has sat on CISA's KEV list since October 28 with a November 14 federal due date. CVSS: Not specified by source (Apple publishes none; Google's v4 rating for the Brotli flaw is 9.2).
**Why it matters:** KEV-listed, actively exploited flaws landing in Apple's bundled libraries eventually surface in Apple builds — the Node2Shell fix closes that exposure on Apple platforms even though there is no indication of attacks against Apple products specifically.
**Action:** install the November 9–10 builds on all managed iPhones, iPads, Macs and Watches; the same 27.2.1/26.7.3/15.8.3 builds also drop compromised eMudhra/emSign roots from the system trust store.

### Microsoft fixes Exchange sign-ins broken by October Windows Server updates
**Event date:** November 11, 2026 (BleepingComputer, 4:07 AM ET). *Update on previously reported:* `ws-update-exchange-break` (first reported October 19–20 with "fix pending"). Microsoft said the November Patch Tuesday cumulative updates — KB5131080 and KB5131090 — resolve the OWA sign-in failures, stuck Outbox mail and mailbox-replication errors that hit on-premises Exchange Server on Windows Server 2019 and later after the October security updates. Root cause: a new default cap on memory the RPC Endpoint Mapper uses when handling Endpoint Lookup calls, introduced by the October updates. For shops that can't deploy immediately, Microsoft published a workaround — raise the cap via `reg add "HKLM\SOFTWARE\Microsoft\Rpc" /v EpmapSavedContextCap /t REG_DWORD /d 65536 /f` on the Exchange server, reboot, and monitor. CVSS: Not applicable (regression fix, not a security flaw).
**Why it matters:** the October regression forced admins into an awkward choice — keep a broken OWA or roll back security updates; a cumulative fix that preserves October's patches ends that trade-off three-plus weeks later.
**Action:** deploy KB5131080/KB5131090 to affected Exchange servers, or apply the EpmapSavedContextCap registry workaround plus reboot until you can.

Sources:

### November cumulative updates resolve September's USB audio breakage
**Event date:** November 11, 2026 (BleepingComputer, 6:18 AM ET; Microsoft Release Health update). Microsoft confirmed the November 10 Patch Tuesday cumulative update (KB5132061 and later) fixes the USB Audio Class 1.0 failures that have plagued Windows systems since the September security updates — devices failing to start with Device Manager "Code 10" errors, dead volume controls, and silence in multichannel modes. The breakage spans client and server: Windows 10, Windows 11 and Windows Server 2012 and later; Microsoft's September 14 out-of-band fixes only covered the 8-channel/3D-audio symptoms, leaving the rest until now. Users who can't patch report temporarily restoring audio by switching to 2-channel mode; Microsoft has not published an official workaround. CVSS: Not applicable (regression fix).
**Why it matters:** a month of silent USB audio on headsets, DACs and conference hardware is exactly the kind of unglamorous outage that erodes trust in Patch Tuesday — and drives risky update-deferral behavior.
**Action:** install the November 10 cumulative updates on affected PCs and servers.

## Attacks and campaigns

### Maersk investigates data theft from a legacy post-acquisition system
**Event date:** November 11, 2026 (Maersk statement and Reuters, Copenhagen). A.P. Moller-Maersk said Wednesday it is investigating "an IT security incident involving unauthorised access and data extraction from one of our local legacy systems from a previous acquisition." The company said the incident is isolated to that local system, found no indication of impact on other Maersk systems or on customer/partner integrations (APIs, EDI), that its findings "so far strongly suggest no sensitive data has been disclosed," and that it does not anticipate disruption — booking and shipment platforms are operating normally. Maersk asked customers to treat unexpected communications claiming to relate to the incident with caution and to verify through their usual Maersk contact. The Register's write-up ran at 12:05 UTC; a RansomLook/Whitearmor leak-site claim naming Maersk dates back to November 7 and remains unconfirmed. CVSS: Not applicable (no CVE; incident disclosure).
**Why it matters:** every shipping line runs sprawls of acquired, lightly-maintained legacy systems — the exact soft tissue attackers sit in while the flagship network hardens. Post-NotPetya, any Maersk intrusion draws supply-chain scrutiny, and the "verify through your usual contact" line is a deliberate anti-phishing shield against follow-on scam campaigns riding the news.
**Action:** trade partners should treat Maersk-branded messages about this incident as suspect until verified through named contacts, and watch for credential-harvesting waves impersonating the carrier.

### Kazakhstan-linked Silent Lynx ran Tajik government espionage with the Hermes AI-agent framework
**Event date:** November 11, 2026 (CloudSek report published Wednesday; Reuters, Washington, 6:07 AM ET). Singapore-based CloudSek said it caught the "Silent Lynx" group — already tied by other researchers to Kazakhstan — using **Hermes, an open-source framework for autonomous AI agents**, to steal more than 2,000 national-security-related documents from neighboring Tajikistan after the spies accidentally left their tasking server exposed to the internet. Reconnaissance with Hermes began around mid-August; a couple of weeks later the agent broke into Tajikistan's electronic document system, then wrote a custom program to download, organize and chunk the files for exfiltration. "Instead of typing hacking commands by hand, the attacker told Hermes what to do, and Hermes planned the attacks, wrote the rules, ran them, kept notes, and wrote up reports," CloudSek told Reuters; recovered instructions included the system prompt "You are an elite offensive security specialist and red teamer." CloudSek said the operators spoke Russian, worked Kazakh business hours, and often reached the server over a residential Kazakh connection. CVSS: Not applicable (actor campaign).
**Why it matters:** this is the clearest documented case yet of an espionage-motivated — not merely criminal — team delegating an entire intrusion lifecycle to an autonomous agent framework, echoing the ARTEX campaigns from October and confirming defenders' fear that agent toolkits are crossing from cybercrime into state-adjacent intel collection.
**Action:** security teams consuming open-weight agent frameworks should treat operator tasking files, agent-generated scripts and note-taking artifacts as first-class hunt pivots, and block egress to ad-hoc residential-IP C2 like the exposed tasking server.

### eMudhra revokes 8 more CAs it had declared unaffected; Microsoft to strip last eMudhra roots November 16
**Event date:** November 11, 2026 (The Hacker News; revocations completed "late last week," full incident report posted to Mozilla's bug tracker this week). *Update on previously reported:* `emudhra-*` October key-compromise entries. The in-house Python script behind eMudhra's October key compromise cost the company eight more intermediate CAs — not because their keys were weak, but because the script pulled their RSA keys out of Utimaco HSMs into ceremony-machine memory during an April 30, 2025 practice transfer and eMudhra "cannot show that the keys were handled as required by its own rules." The same readback line had truncated the original eight ECDSA keys to their leading byte (THN computed all eight values — the smallest was 4, the largest 237); the flawed CAs passed linting and two WebTrust audit cycles. Six of the newly revoked CAs issued TLS certs, two code-signing, with 195 certificates chained to them (29 to eMudhra's own domains). Timeline update: Microsoft begins removing the 10 remaining eMudhra roots (including emSign Root CA - G1) from Windows trust on November 16; Chrome/Firefox distrust post-November-16 G1 certificates; Apple ends all G1 trust November 30, and its new 27.2.1/26.7.3/15.8.3 builds stop bundled curl/git from accepting the compromised TLS CA certificates. CVSS: Not applicable.
**Why it matters:** the count of "acceptable risk" CAs keeps shrinking as the audit unravels — process violations alone are enough to revoke trust — and the November 16/30 trust cliffs now have hard dates to engineer around.
**Action:** inventory any certificate chains or code-signing paths reaching eMudhra/emSign roots before Microsoft's November 16 removal and Apple's November 30 cutoff; test TLS and signing workflows on the patched Apple builds.

### iVerify: leaked iPhone exploit kits are now a criminal-business, delivered via Instagram ads
**Event date:** November 11, 2026 (Help Net Security on iVerify's 2026 Advanced Mobile Threat Report). iVerify's report documents criminals running the **DarkSword** and **Coruna** iPhone exploit kits — tools with nation-state lineage that leaked publicly (a DarkSword variant hit GitHub within a week of its March disclosure) — against everyday users: one infection chain started when an Instagram ad simply loaded while the user scrolled. A key path ran through "DeadTag," a traffic-selling operation that took over an expired e-commerce-analytics domain still referenced in store templates, routing from a network of thousands of sites pulling code from 660+ re-registered domains with an estimated 135 million monthly visits; one destination fingerprinted Safari on iOS 18.4.0–18.7.2 and fired the DarkSword chain. Credential theft (Keychain extraction) and crypto-wallet harvesting drove three-quarters of detections; roughly 1.07 per 1,000 monitored consumer iOS devices showed spyware indicators; every enterprise DarkSword/Coruna detection involved an employee at or below managing-director level; and iVerify confirmed infections on devices that never received Apple threat notifications. Reboots or updates can erase the evidence before an investigation starts. CVSS: Not specified by source.
**Why it matters:** this is the commoditization floor for iOS attacks — no zero-click spyware vendor needed, just ad networks and stale analytics tags reaching corporate email and SSO sessions on BYOD iPhones.
**Action:** move iPhones past the 18.4.0–18.7.2 targeting window, scrub template references to expired third-party analytics domains, and factor reboot-wipes into mobile IR evidence capture.

## Defensive tools and advisories

### SANS 2026 State of ICS/OT Security: one in three industrial organizations hit; detection regressing
**Event date:** November 11, 2026 (SANS Institute release; Infosecurity Magazine same day). SANS' ninth annual survey (nearly 700 respondents, energy-led, with critical manufacturing/IT/government) finds **34% of organizations experienced a confirmed ICS/OT cyber incident in 2026 — the largest single-year jump in the survey's nine-year history** — and **72% of those incidents caused facility or system downtime**, with safety impacts nearly doubling year over year. Detection got worse for the first time on record: only **41% of incidents were caught within 24 hours** (down from >50% in prior years), while 43% cite budget as the top barrier to expanding ICS/OT detection even though ICS-native platforms catch more incidents (38%) than any other method. Manual-operation readiness is an untested black box: only 15% can run manually past 48 hours without centralized SCADA/DCS and 40% can't say; just 13% report full IT-OT visibility across the ICS Cyber Kill Chain, and 16% have fully woven ICS/OT resilience into BC/DR. Organizations that test plans annually and integrate them into BC/DR restore critical facilities same-day 46% of the time, versus 27% overall. CVSS: Not applicable (survey).
**Why it matters:** as AI-accelerated attack tempo compresses dwell-time expectations, the survey shows the OT estate is moving the wrong way on the one metric that matters pre-escalation — time to detect — while resilience remains paper-based.
**Action:** run SANS' free ICS Cyber Resilience Scorecard against your program; prioritize tested (not written) manual-mode procedures and ICS-native detection budgets.
