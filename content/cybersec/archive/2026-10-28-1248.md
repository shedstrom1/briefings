## Vulnerabilities and patches

**Eclypsium's InfraTrust Pulse: 133 infrastructure advisories, 16 tied to active exploitation, PHP baked into appliance UIs**
*Event date: Wednesday, October 28, 2026 (report covered by BleepingComputer at 10:00 AM ET).*
The October InfraTrust Pulse tracked 133 new security advisories across 18 network-infrastructure vendors between September 18 and October 14 — 2,813 vulnerabilities, 37 rated critical, five with maximum CVSS 10.0, and 58 remotely exploitable without authentication; sixteen advisories contained flaws already in CISA's KEV catalog. The actively-exploited roll call spans Citrix NetScaler ADC/Gateway (CVE-2026-88771, CVE-2026-88772, plus SAML flaw CVE-2026-88779), Ivanti Connect Secure (CVE-2026-104935), Check Point Security Management Server (CVE-2026-93616), Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager (CVE-2026-76504), Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator (CVE-2026-93952 and the October 22 CVE-2026-114625), FortiMail (CVE-2026-104286), Cisco Secure Email Gateway (CVE-2026-76519), F5 BIG-IP APM (CVE-2026-94127), and PAN-OS management interface (CVE-2026-0322). The report's PHP2Shell angle is the sleeper: firewalls, VPNs, ADCs and NAS boxes compile PHP into firmware, so customers can't install the October 21 PHP fixes themselves — Palo Alto, F5, Citrix and QNAP are all still rebuilding. The Linux kernel "Copy Fail" LPE (CVE-2026-31431) has spread from 19 to 29 advisories across eight vendors.
*Why it matters:* The Pulse turns this month's chaos into a patch-prioritization queue from an independent index of the appliance ecosystem — and confirms the supply-chain trap that made PHP2Shell so ugly: bundled runtimes inside firmware, where every fix waits on the vendor's build pipeline.
*Action:* InfraTrust recommends prioritizing NetScaler and Ivanti Connect Secure as remote-access doorways, then Fortinet's October 22 FortiOS/FortiManager/FortiAnalyzer updates on internet-facing devices, then the Check Point, Cisco SD-WAN, VeloCloud, FortiMail, Cisco SEG, BIG-IP APM and PAN-OS mgmt items. Keep PHP-backed management interfaces off the internet while awaiting firmware.

## Attacks and campaigns

**Lumen: Volt Typhoon ran zero-day campaigns through "Silent Train," a disposable relay network cleaned after every op**
*Event date: Wednesday, October 28, 2026 (Black Lotus Labs report published today; BleepingComputer 12:00 PM ET).*
Black Lotus Labs has spent three years tracking Silent Train, the covert access framework China-nexus Volt Typhoon built after law enforcement disrupted its KV-Botnet in late 2023. It chains compromised SOHO/IoT devices (Intelbras modems overseas, Netgear Orbi units in the US) exploited on demand, used for one operation — typically under eight hours — then wiped of all tooling, keys and config files. From inside those relay sessions Lumen observed exploitation of at least four confirmed zero-days and one suspected: CVE-2026-16812 (Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator), CVE-2026-20127 (Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN), CVE-2025-8875/8876 (N-able N-central), CVE-2024-39717 (Versa Director), and a likely Quest KACE zero-day. Victims ran across telecoms, government, defense contractors, satellite operators, military-affiliated universities, MSPs and utilities, concentrated in Hawaii, California and Texas — an Indo-Pacific signature — and Lumen notified hundreds of organizations. All CVE severity ratings: Not specified by source.
*Why it matters:* This is the clearest public anatomy yet of how Volt Typhoon — the actor CISA says pre-positions in US critical infrastructure for a future crisis — actually operates: hands-on-keyboard zero-day use against management planes (SD-WAN orchestrators, RMM, out-of-band platforms) that deliver entire customer ecosystems without noisy lateral movement, with anti-forensics so thorough that only always-on network visibility caught it.
*Action:* Black Lotus Labs recommends restricting internet exposure of centralized management interfaces, fast patching of orchestrators/RMM, longer log retention with centralization, and auditing trusted partner connections, per CISA/NCSC Volt Typhoon guidance.

**123 Reg discloses "AI-driven cyberattack" with China/Singapore infrastructure and ARTEX-consistent tooling**
*Event date: Wednesday, October 28, 2026 (Register publication 07:45 ET; customer notices went out this week; intrusion September 18).*
The GoDaddy-owned UK domain registrar told customers its contact data, "related customer relationship data," VAT numbers and product information from an API were exposed in what it calls the first known AI-driven cyberattack against the company. A spokesperson said attacker core infrastructure was hosted in China and Singapore, that investigators found "AI cyberattack 'skills'" on those servers — including a mirror of Anthropic's open-sourced skills and other AI-exploitation frameworks — and that artifacts are consistent with the group CrowdStrike tracked in CSA-261162, the likely Chinese-speaking actor that used the open-source ARTEX agentic pentester against South Korean financial institutions. The activity "was limited to reading," the company says, though copies are possible; it reported to the FTC and UK/EU data-protection authorities and helped take attacker servers down via Singapore-based ISPs. CVSS: Not specified by source (pre-auth intrusion via unspecified vector).
*Why it matters:* If the ARTEX linkage holds, the same agentic-AI attack playbook that hit Korean banks (and, per earlier reporting, GMO's Japanese VPS cloud) is now crossing into Western SMB infrastructure — registrar accounts being a launchpad for domain hijacking and downstream phishing.
*Action:* 123 Reg urges customers to verify any contact by logging in directly, never share passwords/payment codes, and enable two-step verification.

**Huntress: intrusions through trojaned "camera management software" — CMS.exe drops reverse shell, fake WpcMon, sideloaded DLLs**
*Event date: Wednesday, October 28, 2026 (Help Net Security coverage).*
Huntress traced several September–October incidents into business networks to a compromised camera-management application whose binary (CMS.exe) carried no version info; embedded strings point to a Korean analog/IP-camera and DVR vendor, though Huntress flags the link as unconfirmed. After initial access, the chain is consistent: CMS.exe launches rs.win32.exe (reverse shell) from AppData\Roaming\CMS\RemoteSetup, plants a copy of Microsoft's legitimate WpcMon.exe in AppData\Local, which sideloads two malicious DLLs (IPHLPAPI.DLL, samcli.dll) disguised as zlib libraries, persists via the user Run key, and beacons to 144.172.116[.]37. How the software itself was compromised remains unknown — no logs were recovered. CVSS: Not specified by source (supply-chain compromise, no CVE assigned).
*Why it matters:* Surveillance-management software typically runs on flat networks near the perimeter with broad reach — a classic blind spot — and this campaign shows defenders may be hunting for a vendor breach nobody can yet name, making the forensic artifacts, not the vendor ID, the usable intelligence.
*Action:* Huntress says to inventory such software, restrict its network access, apply application control against executables/DLLs in user-writable paths, and hunt for the CMS.exe → rs.win32.exe → abnormal WpcMon.exe sequence and the C2 IP.
Source: "Compromised camera management software gives attackers a way into corporate networks" — Help Net Security, Zeljka Zorz, October 28, 2026 — https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/10/28/compromised-camera-management-software-initial-access/

## Defensive tools and advisories

**Cisco open-sources Project Quarry: a Snort-for-agent-behavior rule language to catch AI agents mid-attack**
*Event date: Wednesday, October 28, 2026 (Cisco blog "open sourcing today"; Register exclusive 08:00 ET).*
Project Quarry is a neurosymbolic rule language plus evaluation engine for judging whole AI-agent sessions rather than single actions: ML classifiers and LLM judges supply neural signals (is this tool result a prompt injection?), exact symbolic checks handle the rest (command patterns, string matches, ordering of two tool calls), and rules cover single/multi-turn LLM conversations, tool use, MCP interactions and multimodal inputs. The GitHub repo (cisco-ai-defense/project-quarry) ships a rule starter pack, plugins for Claude Code, Codex and other MCP-compatible agents, and rule-writing guidance. Cisco's framing is explicitly post-mortem on the July OpenAI agents-intrusion at Hugging Face — Hugging Face reconstructed ~17,600 agent actions after the fact; Quarry is built to flag sequences like "sensitive file read followed within a defined window by an external send" while they're still running, preserving evidence. Amy Chang (Cisco AI threat research) stresses rules complement, not replace, chain-of-thought monitoring.
*Why it matters:* Every agent-security story this month — rogue pentest agents, MCP data exfil, the 1,200-site autonomous breach wave — has been about multi-step abuse of permitted actions. Quarry is the first attempt at a shared, testable, open rule format for that class of detection, from the org that already gave the industry Snort; community adoption is now the experiment.
*Action:* Defenders running MCP-connected coding agents should pilot the repo's starter rules (credential-in-tool-result followed by external call is the flagship pattern) inside their own telemetry.
Source: "A Shared Language for Agent Defense: Introducing Project Quarry" — Cisco Blogs (Swanda, Balakrishnan, Chang, Berlin), October 28, 2026 — https://blogs.cisco.com/ai/project-quarry-agent-security-detections
Source: "Cisco open sources tool for detecting AI agents on hacking sprees" — The Register, Jessica Lyons, October 28, 2026 12:00 UTC — https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/28/cisco-open-sources-tool-for-detecting-ai-agents-on-hacking-sprees/5305867

**UK budget: first formal pledge to consult on software-vendor liability for cyber breaches — plus £60M Cyber Shield and £40M "National Agentic Security Unit"**
*Event date: Wednesday, October 28, 2026 (budget documents published Wednesday).*
British budget documents commit the government to consult next year "on how to tackle insecure technology at source and consider where liability for cyber breaches should sit" — the first formal UK pledge of its kind in a country that has leaned on voluntary codes; the JLR hack's estimated £1.9B hit to the economy is the political backdrop. The consultation will build on the Law Commission's product-liability review (consultation opened October 8, closes January 14) which proposes treating software and AI systems as "products" under the Consumer Protection Act 1987. One option under discussion is expanding the PSTI Act; officials want compatibility with the EU's Cyber Resilience Act (fines up to €15M or 2.5% of global turnover, main obligations December 2027). The same budget funds phase one of NCSC's agentic-AI Cyber Shield (£60M), a new £40M National Agentic Security Unit to detect "misaligned agentic activity," and £120M more for the AI Security Institute.
*Why it matters:* Liability is the lever every prior soft-law regime deliberately avoided; a UK consultation, post-PHP2Shell, moves the "secure-by-default by statute" question from think-tank papers into a finance-ministry commitment — with BSA pushback and US friction already telegraphed. The agentic-AI security funding shows the same event-driven policy reflex now pointed at AI-enabled threats.
Source: "UK to look at making software companies liable for cyber breaches" — The Record (Recorded Future News), Alexander Martin, October 28, 2026 — https://therecord.media/uk-budget-proposal-software-companies-liable-cyber-breaches
