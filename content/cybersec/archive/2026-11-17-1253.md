## Attacks and campaigns

**NSA's classified internal network "NSA Net" goes down; no evidence of attack so far** — Event date: Tuesday, November 17 (outage began before CNN's 10:31 AM ET publication).
The highly classified network the National Security Agency uses for internal communications is down, according to six people familiar with the matter, CNN reports; the agency has enacted staggered arrivals for personnel while it works to understand the scope. Sources told CNN there is no evidence of a cyberattack at this point, and current and former US officials said the outage would not affect the agency's ability to communicate urgent intelligence to the president, who is traveling in Asia with multiple backup networks in play. The story lands days after the FAA's nationwide ground stop (traced to a single router in Arkansas) and the 27-hour Microsoft 365 outage (Microsoft ruled out an attack Monday, citing a July gateway bug and a capacity change).
**Why it matters:** Three cascading failures in national-security-adjacent infrastructure in ten days — FAA, M365, now NSA Net — are shaping the threat picture even without hostile action: DHS Secretary Mullin floated an AI-chaos theory on social media, and defenders should expect outage-shaped risk (comms fallback discipline, phishing that exploits the news) rather than a single smoking gun. Watch for follow-on FBI/CISA advisories if the root cause turns out to be anything other than benign.
**Action:** No official action stated; organizations relying on federal comms interfaces should validate their own out-of-band escalation paths while the outage is unresolved.

**Huntress: month-long "blind spray" campaign against MSP ScreenConnect servers using patched CVE-2026-84869** — Event date: Tuesday, November 17 (Huntress analysis reported today; activity ongoing since Oct 20).
Huntress reports attackers have blasted at least 688,000 guest "run file" requests for `update*.bat` at 42 managed-service-provider ScreenConnect servers from 29 IP addresses since October 20, exploiting CVE-2026-84869 (CVSS 9.9), a missing-authorization/improper-privilege-management flaw ConnectWise patched September 8 and CISA put on the KEV catalog September 11. When a technician joins a session, the pushed batch file phones a Telegram bot and pulls a rogue ScreenConnect client from masterdarky[.]com — one chain completed in about nine seconds, with a fake "Windows Update" client spawned silently, no prompt shown to the technician. Huntress explicitly distinguishes this campaign from the SCKit toolkit activity and the crypto-exchange-impersonation session abuse reported earlier this month (ledger: SCKit-ScreenConnect-MSP-campaign, CVE-2026-84869).
**Why it matters:** This is industrialized RMM reverse-infection: an MSP technician workstation holds consoles and saved credentials for every customer, so 42 vulnerable servers are 42 potential multi-tenant breaches. The "wait for any technician to join" pattern means exposure is a ticking clock, not a one-shot probe.
**Action (per Huntress/ConnectWise):** Update technician ScreenConnect host clients to version 26.6.5 or later and all Access agents ConnectWise lists; audit servers that logged guest "run file" requests since October 20 and hunt rogue clients.

## Vulnerabilities and patches

**Oracle ships its scheduled November 17 security update for 11 Java SE flaws as unconfirmed "Java4Shell" (CVE-2026-114938) leak churns** — Event date: Tuesday, November 17 (scheduled monthly Critical Security Patch Update; build listings refreshed today).
Oracle's monthly patch cycle (third Tuesday of November under the regime it introduced in May) landed today with fixes for 11 Java SE vulnerabilities, all rated by Oracle as remotely exploitable without authentication, maximum CVSS 3.1 score 8.1 — while a separate, unverified report of a pre-auth RCE dubbed "Java4Shell" (CVE-2026-114938, reportedly CVSS 4.0 9.2, memory corruption in OpenJDK 17.0.6+ since January 2023) circulated on the back of security firm Calif's claim that it found and reported the flaw and that details leaked; Calif promised its write-up by 1:00 PM Pacific today, after Oracle patches. watchTowr acknowledged the reports but cautioned the technical details remain unconfirmed; the CVE record is reserved with no description, and the OpenJDK Vulnerability Group has published nothing newer than October 24 — so it is not established whether Java4Shell is among today's 11 fixes. Oracle's ops.java replacement-build listings (8u513, 11.0.33.1, 17.0.21.1, 21.0.13.1, 25.0.5.1, 27.0.1.1) went live around midday ET while the formal advisory was still pending; affected products span Java SE 8/11/17/21/25/27, GraalVM for JDK 17/21, GraalVM EE 21.3 and JavaFX.
**Why it matters:** Java4Shell is a PHP2Shell-shaped scenario in progress — a named, high-score runtime RCE with leaked-but-unverified scope (a decade-plus of Java 17 deployments), plus Korea's NCSC reportedly pushing an inspection advisory to public institutions. The disciplined read today: patch the 11 confirmed Oracle fixes, treat the 9.2 as unvalidated, and wait for Calif's write-up and the OpenJDK group's advisory before mass emergency action — while starting the runtime inventory now, because finding every bundled JDK in containers is the slow part.
**Severity (as published):** CVE-2026-114938: CVSS 4.0 9.2 per reports (unconfirmed by vendor/NVD). Oracle's 11 Java SE flaws: max CVSS 3.1 8.1, all pre-auth remote.
**Action (per Oracle/THN):** Plan moves off current builds to 8u513 / 11.0.33.1 / 17.0.21.1 / 21.0.13.1 / 25.0.5.1 / 27.0.1.1; inventory every Java runtime including container base images; patch internet-facing services first; track Temurin/Corretto/Zulu builds separately.
