## Vulnerabilities and patches

**Update on previously reported — PHP2Shell (CVE-2026-109618 / CVE-2026-109619, CVSS 9.2): botnet recruitment attempts, attacker tooling leaks, and a demonstrated ASLR bypass.** Event date: exploit attempts and disclosures observed Oct 22–23; coverage published Oct 23.
Sophos observed unsuccessful attempts to exploit the two PHP2Shell flaws to deploy "Potassium," an expanded Mirai-derived botnet — its earlier report of a successful customer-environment exploit has been corrected to "scanning and a small number of failed exploitation attempts." The Potassium payload phones home to 134[.]199.154[.]64 over TCP/50792 (after resolving thunder.godofthunder[.]st), expects an ELF second stage, and its end goal is volumetric DDoS against two adjacent hosts (45.88.138[.]0 / .44) in a single Ukrainian network; Arctic Wolf, tracking the same campaign, found ARM and x86 builds with (disabled) Ethereum Name Service C2 resolution and 24 flood commands. Separately, SOCRadar found a "one-click" PHP2Shell exploit for PHP 8.4.26/Apache mod_php (`aslr_exploit.py`) plus three preconditions-screening scripts sitting on an attacker server (206.188.197[.]105:9999), with 10 open directories distributing PHP2Shell tooling overall; Hunt.io says 123 servers were targeted, none exploited. And VulnCheck's exploit-development director told The Hacker News the team built "a novel technique" chaining both CVEs — a session-cookie memory leak to defeat ASLR, then session-upload-progress abuse for command execution — "on x86-64 systems, even with PHP's recommended production error-display setting," saying no other public or commercial exploit does this. U.K. government confirmed 32 digital services unavailable (mostly precautionary shutdowns) and ~50,000 NCSC warnings; Censys detects 34.4 million PHP services still vulnerable as of Oct 23, while VirusTotal counts ~272,000 hosts on supported branches 8.2–8.5.
- *Why it matters:* The street is not yet weaponized — every observed campaign has failed — but the reliability problem is being solved in public (ASLR bypass demonstrated, one-click tooling appearing in open directories). The long tail is the real risk: the vulnerable code dates to PHP 5.4 (2012) and EOL 7.4/8.0/8.1 lines get no fix.
- *Action:* Patch to PHP 8.5.12 / 8.4.27 / 8.3.36 / 8.2.35 now; migrate or backport anything on 7.4/8.0/8.1. Block the published IOCs (attack sources 85.11.167[.]243, 217.60.242[.]76, 45.198.224[.]169; loader 92.118.112[.]100; C2 134.199.154[.]64, 117.55.202[.]115; Wordfence's scanner set).
- Source: The Hacker News, "PHP2Shell Exploit Attempts Hit Millions as Attackers Try to Deploy Mirai Malware," Oct 23, 2026 — https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/php2shell-exploit-attempts-hit-millions.html
- Source: Sophos, "Proof-of-concept exploit released for PHP vulnerabilities (CVE-2026-109618, CVE-2026-109619)" (correction + Potassium IOCs) — https://www.sophos.com/en-us/blog/proof-of-concept-exploit-released-for-php-vulnerabilities-109618-109619

**Update on previously reported — Node2Shell (CVE-2026-111497, High per Node.js): Imperva demonstrates RCE on an official Node.js build.** Event date: Oct 23 (Imperva disclosure; flaw patched Oct 21).
Imperva's Red Team reproduced full code execution by feeding crafted Brotli data to the decoder bundled in the *official* Node.js 24.22.0 Linux arm64 image — no debugger, no custom build (the first public PoC reached code execution only on a deliberately weakened build; on a stock build it produced a crash and limited memory read). It reproduced through both paths: `fetch()` of a Brotli-compressed response and Brotli request-body decoding via Express 5 / body-parser (tested 5.0.0–5.3.0 and 2.0.0–2.3.0). Execution took several tries on that one build; other architectures and the 22.x/26.x lines were not tested; on fixed 24.22.1 the same input was rejected. Root cause: the decoder continues past an impossible block-type change in a Brotli stream, writing out of bounds; Node.js fixed it by importing an upstream Brotli patch. No in-the-wild exploitation reported as of Oct 22. Vercel deployed inbound-blocking rules Oct 21 (they don't cover fetched Brotli responses).
- *Why it matters:* This closes the "is it actually exploitable on stock builds?" question with "yes" — and Express 5's new Brotli request-body support widens the reachable surface to any route with body parsing. There is no in-runtime workaround; only the update removes the flaw.
- *Action:* Upgrade to Node.js 22.23.4 / 24.22.1 / 26.12.1 — and rebuild container images and bundled-Node artifacts that a package inventory will miss; move anything on 20.x or earlier to a supported line.
- Source: The Hacker News, "Researchers Demonstrate Node2Shell RCE Exploit on Node.js Build Using Crafted Brotli Data," Oct 23, 2026 — https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/researchers-demonstrate-node2shell-rce.html
- Source: Imperva, "Node2Shell: From Brotli Data to Remote Code Execution" — https://www.imperva.com/blog/node2shell-from-brotli-data-to-remote-code-execution/

**x402 payment SDKs: 25 flaws, three critical — including full-balance auto-signing; August fixes carried no advisory.** Event date: V12 disclosure Oct 22 (publication time not stated on the post); The Hacker News' independently verified report Oct 23.
Security firm V12 audited the x402 standard's SDKs (Coinbase-born, now a Linux Foundation project backed by Visa and Mastercard) and found 25 vulnerabilities, three rated critical: (1) the default client set no spending limit, so a hostile API could turn one 402 response into a signature authorizing the wallet's *entire* balance of a supported token; (2) the TypeScript Solana facilitator parsed a transaction's third instruction without checking the instruction type, so an `ApproveChecked` (moves nothing) was accepted as a completed payment — "a complete payment bypass"; (3) the Sign-In-With-X flow signed login messages without checking that the message's domain/URI matched the contacted site, letting a malicious server harvest a reusable login signature for another site. All three were fixed in the Aug 18 releases (spend now capped at $1 to recognized stablecoins by default) — but with no security advisory, and as of Oct 23 The Hacker News found npm audit and PyPI list no advisories for the affected versions. No exploitation has been reported.
- *Why it matters:* Agents that auto-sign payments were running funded and uncapped against arbitrary servers, and dependency scanners still won't tell you — the quiet-fix pattern is exactly how agent-payment infrastructure rots.
- *Action:* Upgrade @x402/fetch, @x402/axios, @x402/core, @x402/svm, @x402/extensions to ≥2.23.0 (latest 2.28.0), Go module ≥2.28.0, Python ≥2.25.0; never pass `spendControls: false`; verify SIWx domain/URI before signing; set per-payment and total budgets.
- Source: V12, "25 Vulnerabilities in the x402 Protocol: Inside Three Critical Findings," Oct 22, 2026 — https://v12.sh/blog/x402
- Source: The Hacker News, "Critical x402 SDK Flaw Could Let Malicious APIs Drain AI Agents' Token Balances," Oct 23, 2026 — https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/critical-x402-sdk-flaw-could-let.html

## Attacks and campaigns

**Iran's Prince of Persia APT hosts C2 inside the U.S. for the first time since 2016 after its longest-ever blackout.** Event date: Semperis research published to SecurityWeek Oct 23, 02:39 ET.
Semperis's Tomer Bar reports that Prince of Persia (aka Infy) went silent for three months starting late February — the longest confirmed downtime in its ~20-year history, overlapping the Iran–U.S./Israel hostilities that began Feb 28 — then restarted operations on May 27 with a new C2 and resumed data theft; within seven days its Tonnerre malware siphoned over 1,800 files from a single victim. In August Bar located a Tornado second-stage server (likely built July 7) on **Namecheap shared hosting in the United States** — the group's first U.S.-hosted C2 since 2016 — a deliberate move off its usual VPS pattern onto hosting packed with thousands of legitimate domains; by Aug 24 the group was standing up a replacement hidden behind Cloudflare, apparently aware of the exposure. Tornado steals files via a Telegram bot; Semperis has published IoCs.
- *Why it matters:* An Iranian collection APT blending into benign U.S. shared hosting flips the usual geolocation-blocking assumption and will trip threat-hunting playbooks keyed to "Iranian" netblocks; the record-lull-then-resume pattern also suggests the group is intact and re-tooling, not degraded.
- *Action:* Ingest Semperis's IoCs, and hunt for anomalous Telegram traffic and Tornado second-stage beacons to shared-hosting IPs — don't rely on geo-IP exclusion rules.
- Source: SecurityWeek, "Iran's Prince of Persia APT Hosts C&C in US for First Time Since 2016," Eduard Kovacs, Oct 23, 2026 2:39 AM ET — https://www.securityweek.com/irans-prince-of-persia-apt-hosts-cc-in-us-for-first-time-since-2016/
- Source: Semperis, "Prince of Persia: A Threat Group Hiding in Plain Sight" — https://www.semperis.com/blog/prince-of-persia-threat-group-hiding-in-plain-sight/
