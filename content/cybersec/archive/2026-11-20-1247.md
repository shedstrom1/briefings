## Vulnerabilities and patches

**WhatsApp fixes ad-reply validation flaw abused in scam campaigns (CVE-2026-23871)** — Event date: November 20, 2026 (advisory covered in-window; update pushed to WhatsApp/WhatsApp Business for Android 2.26.17.4).
WhatsApp's own security team found an incomplete-validation bug in how Click-to-WhatsApp ad-reply URLs are handled: if an attacker sent media from an attacker-controlled URL and rendered a crafted ad-reply message in a thread, the target's device could reveal its IP information to the attacker. A WhatsApp spokesperson told SecurityWeek the team found the issue "while disrupting a scam campaign, and we believe a scammer was using it to make their scam message seem more credible." WhatsApp also shipped two medium-impact fixes — CVE-2026-102769 (SQL injection via a crafted "order details" message from an unofficial client, Android, fixed in 2.26.35.74) and CVE-2026-44908 (a remote-attestation verifier bypass affecting iOS/Android/Mac, which Meta says the binary-transparency ledger shows was never exploited). Severity: low impact per WhatsApp's advisory for 23871; medium for the other two; no CVSS scores published by source.
Why it matters: IP disclosure looks minor, but it lets fraud operators confirm a target is live and reachable, hardening social-engineering pipelines before the actual scam lands. That a mainstream messenger is now finding in-the-wild scam abuse during takedowns is the signal.
Action: update WhatsApp and WhatsApp Business for Android to 2.26.17.4 or later.
Source: SecurityWeek, "WhatsApp Fixes Flaw Uncovered While Disrupting Scam Campaign," November 20, 2026, 11:01 AM ET — https://www.securityweek.com/whatsapp-fixes-flaw-uncovered-while-disrupting-scam-campaign/

**KVM flaw let a guest VM read ~127 GB of host memory on AMD cloud hosts (CVE-2026-99312)** — Event date: November 20, 2026 (The Hacker News published 07:20:00 EST, one minute before this window opened; the 07:20 edition carried no KVM item, so it is included here rather than lost).
A flaw in the Linux KVM hypervisor, found by Terra Security researcher Oren Yomtov and reported through DigitalOcean's bug bounty, allowed reading roughly 127 GB of a shared server's memory from inside the attacker's own Droplet — memory that on multi-tenant hosts can include other customers' VMs. It affects only AMD-based hosts with nested virtualization enabled. Fixed kernels shipped upstream Tuesday (the day before disclosure); DigitalOcean live-patched its fleet and told Droplet customers "no action needed." Severity: CVSS score not specified by source; the provider internally treated it as critical.
Why it matters: cross-tenant memory disclosure is the classic cloud-isolation failure mode, and this one required no guest compromise beyond renting a Droplet. Operators running their own AMD KVM fleets — especially nested-virt lab and CI hosts — are the exposed population.
Action: update host kernels or disable nested virtualization on internet-exposed AMD KVM hosts.

## Attacks and campaigns

**Bazuka: a rentable Android fraud framework with screenshot overlays and an LLM "victim scorer"** — Event date: November 20, 2026 (ThreatFabric technical report published; The Hacker News coverage 07:58 EST).
ThreatFabric discovered Bazuka in late October while investigating an NFC-relay banking trojan and describes it as "a full malware framework": a dropper/downloader, a core, and 23 modules spanning overlays, keylogging, 2FA/SMS interception, remote control, crypto-address swapping, and contactless-card reads. Its standout trick: the trojan screenshots the home or lock screen and replays it as a frozen overlay to hide malicious activity running underneath. The core enables Wireless Debugging through accessibility-driven menu navigation, intercepts the pairing PIN, and opens an ADB shell; it also null-routes traffic and disables Play Protect components during staging, with a self-destruct option. The operator panel integrates LLMs (local or API-hosted) — sample prompts ask the model to score victims' attractiveness as targets — and sells out to "traffers" who drive installs via ads and bogus sites, including a fake premium TikTok dropper. Severity: CVSS not specified by source (malware research, not a CVE).
Why it matters: this is Android banking-malware-as-a-service industrializing the playbook iOS spyware used — AI triage of victims, modular tasking, and an overlay evasion that defeats what users actually see on screen. Mobile MDM and app-attestation stacks that don't check Wireless Debugging state have a new failure mode.
Action: flag devices with Developer options/Wireless Debugging enabled; hunt package names com.tiktok.premium / com.tiktok.premium.pro; review Play-integrity signals for sideload-heavy fleets.
Sources: ThreatFabric, "Bazuka: The Fraud Framework That Can Do It All," November 20, 2026 — https://www.threatfabric.com/blogs/bazuka-the-fraud-framework-that-can-do-it-all ; The Hacker News, November 20, 2026 — https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/bazuka-android-malware-uses-fake-screen.html
