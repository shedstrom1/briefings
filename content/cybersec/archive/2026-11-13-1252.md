## Attacks and campaigns

**Uber confirms thieves stole US drivers' license data via vendor Fireworks AI** — event date: Friday, November 13 (California AG notice dated November 10, public disclosure today).
An unauthorized party used compromised credentials at service provider Fireworks AI to access Uber's account with that provider; the provider told Uber on October 9 and Uber's own investigation confirmed on October 13 that recipients' personal data had been accessed. The notice itself doesn't name the provider, but TechCrunch reported Friday that it is Fireworks AI and that the exposed data includes drivers' license information; state filings so far cover 3,430 South Carolina, 2,567 Massachusetts and 1,873 Rhode Island residents (7,870 total), with DC/MD/NC/NY/PA notices also issued. Uber says it severed data connections with the provider, is offering 90 days of Cyberscout (TransUnion) credit monitoring, and has identified no misuse to date.
*Why it matters:* This is the first publicly named victim fallout of the October Fireworks AI incident (this briefing reported its expansion Nov 9) — an AI-infra vendor breach rippling into ride-hailing PII, and a reminder that "offline" vendor credential caches (the same pattern as the Qubole and Notion/Fireworks threads) become regulator-visible weeks later.
*Action:* Drivers who received notice should enroll in the offered monitoring; enterprises using Fireworks AI should inventory what Uber-style data flows went through it in early October.

**Update on previously reported Apify intrusion (ledger: apify-internal-breach, Nov 12): customer data access confirmed** — event date: Tuesday, November 10; confirmation published Nov 13 07:41 ET.
Apify confirmed its November 9–10 intrusion reached customer accounts: an attacker used one compromised credential to obtain additional credentials, and some customers had personal information or stored data accessed. A leaked notification (via Reddit) lists names, emails, billing details, account activity, IPs, **salted password hashes, 2FA recovery codes, phone numbers and Google/GitHub sign-in OAuth tokens**; card data limited to name/expiry/last-4. Apify has changed critical internal secrets, revoked some OAuth connections, and told customers who build Actors from private GitHub repos to reconnect integrations; account count undisclosed.
*Why it matters:* The upgrade from "tokens rotated" (yesterday's frame) to "recovery codes and OAuth tokens accessed" changes the response from rotate-and-forget to forced re-enrollment — 2FA recovery codes are the classic silent persistence channel.
*Action:* Apify customers: reset passwords, re-issue 2FA (delete old recovery codes), and re-authorize GitHub/Google OAuth even if nothing broke.
Source: SecurityWeek, "Apify Confirms Customer Data Accessed in Cyberattack," Nov 13 07:41 AM ET — https://www.securityweek.com/apify-confirms-customer-data-accessed-in-cyberattack/

**Quantum Systems' Bundeswehr drones back in the air after October cyberattack** — event date: Friday, November 13 (company statement; FT grounding report was Thursday).
The German defense-tech firm said its drone fleet "is operating normally around the globe — including Germany and the Bundeswehr," confirming the military had been the only customer to ground systems as a precaution after an "active cyberattack by hostile actors" detected in October. The Register reports the scare traced to a malicious software update that reportedly reached military servers, which knocked Quantum out of a recent Bundeswehr exercise; the company says there is "no risk in operating" its unmanned systems and that it worked closely with authorities.
*Why it matters:* A supply-chain update path into a defense contractor's customer-facing servers is the scenario the whole October AI-attack wave (ARTEX/Crafty Falcon etc.) has been rehearsing — and the public confirmation + lift-the-grounding is today's milestone.

## Defensive tools and advisories

**Google Project Zero replaces 90+30 with "Full X" countdown disclosure — Full 90 now, Full 60 from Feb 2, 2027, Full 30 from Jan 3, 2028** — event date: Friday, November 13 (announced "Today" on the PZ blog; surfaced on Hacker News 12:17 EST).
Vulnerabilities will now be disclosed a fixed X days after report, no matter the fix state: the grace period and variant policy are abolished, and ITW bugs defer to Google's app-security disclosure policy. The stated driver is AI: LLMs "increased the speed at which attackers can find and exploit vulnerabilities," "zero patch-to-exploitation time is a reasonable, though imperfect, model of attacker behavior," and PZ is now finding "several vulnerabilities a week" where it once found a few a year. Vendors get phased runway because PZ solicited feedback from its highest-volume report targets; hardware exceptions (à la Spectre) remain discretionary.
*Why it matters:* This is the most consequential disclosure-policy shift since 90 days became standard, and it lands on a week when this very briefing has tracked AI-discovered bugs (PHP2Shell, Apache mod_http2 CVE-2026-119618, XSA-523, PostgreSQL's 52-CVE release). Expect other labs and CNA workflows to follow; vendors with slow fix pipelines should renegotiate coordination now.
*Action:* Vendor security leads: re-baseline your MTTR against a 30-day disclosure clock.
Source: Google Project Zero, "Policy and Disclosure: 2026 Edition," Nov 13 — https://projectzero.google/2026/11/disclosure-policy.html ; HN submission 12:17 EST — https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50486581

**Update on previously reported refrigeration wave: Germany's BSI says Danfoss patches still insufficient — take controllers offline or VPN** — event date: Friday, November 13 (BSI advisory; Reuters ~09:20 AM EST).
Germany's federal office for information security (BSI) published a security advisory Friday warning that some of Danish firm Danfoss' industrial-cooling controllers remain vulnerable following the Europe-wide attacks. BSI's guidance: take affected systems offline where possible, otherwise secure any connection via VPN; IT security managers should apply available Danfoss updates, but BSI cautions "even new patches do not guarantee that further vulnerabilities… will not come to light," and industrial control systems "should therefore not be directly accessible from the internet." It follows the US joint fact sheet (reported Nov 5) telling cold-chain operators to "assume targeting."
*Why it matters:* A national cert body openly discounting its own vendor's patch sufficiency is a strong signal to treat ETC/controller exposure as an architectural problem, not a patch-management one.
Source: Reuters, "German watchdog warns some cooling systems remain vulnerable to hacking," Nov 13 ~09:20 AM EST — https://www.reuters.com/world/german-watchdog-warns-some-cooling-systems-remain-vulnerable-hacking-2026-11-13/

**WPSec: reading every WordPress plugin release itself to catch silent security fixes — median advisory lag 6.2 days** — event date: Friday, November 13 (blog post published; HN 12:15 EST).
WPSec's new program diffs every new WordPress.org plugin release hourly with AI models, independent model review, and daily human confirmation, publishing free public advisories (wpsec.com/vuln/). Its measured baseline: advisories appear a median 6.2 days *after* the fix ships (44% over a week, 1-in-7 over a month); of known fixes, 73% were announced as security fixes, 9% used vague wording like "improved validation," and 17% gave no hint at all — those never reach any vulnerability database.
*Why it matters:* For the ~90% of WordPress vulnerabilities that live in plugins, the patch diff is the only timely indicator; "general bug fixes" changelogs are now machine-readable intelligence for attackers. Vendor-side, it's a quiet-fix policy problem waiting to explode.
Source: WPSec blog, "The WordPress Plugin Vulnerabilities Other Scanners Miss," Nov 13 — https://blog.wpsec.com/wordpress-plugin-vulnerabilities-other-scanners-miss/ ; HN — https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50486563
