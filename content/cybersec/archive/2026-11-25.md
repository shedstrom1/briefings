## Vulnerabilities and patches

**Drupal "Fail2Install" (CVE-2026-132165) confirmed exploited in the wild as a full pre-auth RCE chain goes public** — *Update on previously reported* (ledger: Drupal installer advisory, Nov 19 entry; KEV triple add, Nov 24 17:20 edition). Event dates: exploitation on live sites as early as **2026-11-19 19:00 UTC** (Drupal advisory update); Searchlight Cyber exploit analysis and the exploitation confirmation circulated **Nov 25** in-window.

The Drupal Security Team appended to SA-CORE-2026-017 that the flaw "was exploited on live sites as early as 2026-11-19 19:00 UTC." Searchlight Cyber researchers Kevin Gervot and Adam Kues published the mechanism: attackers exhaust ordinary system limits — EMFILE file-descriptor caps, database-connection exhaustion and TCP source-port exhaustion — so that Drupal's installer performs a FAIL, FAIL, SUCCESS sequence on its "is the site already installed?" checks, re-opening `install.php` on a live site after winning a race. Because the installer accepts a SQLite database path with an arbitrary extension, the attacker points it at a `.php` file under the document root, then submits PHP code as the site name — those bytes land inside the SQLite database file, and a path-handling weakness in Apache's `RewriteRule` gets the file executed. The researchers reproduced pre-auth RCE on four common deployment combinations (Apache/PostgreSQL, Apache/MySQL, PHP-FPM/PostgreSQL, PHP-FPM/MySQL) and state Drupal 8.9, 9, 10 and 11 are affected. Fixed versions: Drupal 11.4.13, 11.3.22, 10.6.22 (11.2.x and 10.5.x and below are EOL). CISA added the flaw to KEV on Nov 24 with a three-day remediation deadline of **Nov 27**. Severity as published: **CVSS 9.8** (CISA/press); Drupal's own scale rates it **Highly critical, 20/25**; this is the third Drupal flaw exploited in the wild this year after CVE-2026-9082 (6.5) and CVE-2026-118539 (6.5).
- *Why it matters:* the chain turns availability primitives (ulimits, connection and port exhaustion) into a security boundary crossing, so signature-based detection on "weird but non-malicious" traffic won't catch it, and a Drupal install that survives a database blip can silently re-open its installer. It also shows Anthropic's OSS-scanner-discovered bugs reaching weaponization within days (advisory Nov 18 → exploitation Nov 19 → KEV Nov 24).
- *Action:* upgrade to the fixed branches now; if you cannot, follow Drupal's guidance to secure the installer after installation (Drupal Steward protects known vectors), and treat any unexpected installer traffic or new admin accounts as an incident.

**Palo Alto Networks ships PAN-SA-2026-0015 for actively exploited Fragnesia LPE (CVE-2026-46300)** — *Update on previously reported* (ledger: Fragnesia KEV add Oct 28; PAN-OS management-interface exploitation Oct 28). Event date: bulletin **published November 25, 2026**.

PAN-SA-2026-0015 covers the Linux kernel local privilege escalation dubbed Fragnesia (CVSS **7.8**, vendor-published), a deterministic page-cache corruption bug in the XFRM ESP-in-TCP socket-coalescing path (researcher William Bowling, Zellic/V12 Security; public PoC and Metasploit module exist; EPSS 17.2%). Palo Alto explicitly states: "Exploitation of CVE-2026-46300 has been observed targeting the Palo Alto Networks management interface exposed to the internet." Fixed builds span five branches — 10.2.7-h41, 10.2.10-h45, 10.2.13-h29, 10.2.16-h14, 10.2.18-h15; 11.1.4-h40, 11.1.6-h42, 11.1.7-h14, 11.1.10-h38, 11.1.13-h18, 11.1.16-h6; 11.2.4-h25, 11.2.7-h25, 11.2.10-h19, 11.2.13-h6, 11.2.16-h3; 12.1.4-h15, 12.1.7-h15, 12.1.10-h6; and 12.2.4-h3 — plus later releases. PAN-OS PHP2Shell entry paths were already patched by Oct 30; Palo Alto says the only known PAN-OS exploitation route is PHP2Shell first, then Fragnesia for root.
- *Why it matters:* this closes the privilege-escalation leg of a chain that has been used to root firewalls in AI-assisted intrusions, and it converts "work around it by hiding the management interface" into a patch-or-expose decision before the Thanksgiving holiday window.
- *Action:* install the listed hotfix/branch builds, keep management interfaces off the public internet, and restrict local shell access on unpatched boxes. Until you patch, remember that unpatched PAN-OS with a reachable management interface is still the exposed leg even with PHP2Shell patched upstream in your stack.

**Microsoft's November NTLM hardening for CVE-2026-118737 is breaking third-party authentication** — Event date: confirmed in an updated Microsoft support document and reported **Nov 25 04:02 EST**.

Microsoft confirmed that the November security updates — which fix CVE-2026-118737, a timing-dependent Windows RPC Runtime flaw that lets unauthenticated attackers execute code remotely without user interaction — are the first phase of NTLM message-signing improvements that force authenticated NTLM sessions to establish a cryptographic session key and negotiate real message signing. That change breaks devices and services that misimplement NTLM (for example, an authentication proxy that sets `NTLMSSP_NEGOTIATE_TARGET_INFO` but sends an empty or incomplete TargetInfo field), producing sign-in failures across third-party authentication proxies, SSO solutions and application-packaging tools. There is no fix yet. Diagnostic signature: an `NTLMSSP_CHALLENGE` with an empty "Target Info List" in a network trace, or "Not Found" values in Target Machine event-log fields. Microsoft's interim workaround is to set `ClientAllowUnsignedNtlm` (REG_DWORD = 1) under `HKLM\SYSTEM\CurrentControlSet\Control\Lsa\MSV1_0`, prefer Kerberos where the product supports it, or push the vendor for a compliant update — and it warns the registry key is short-term and may be removed. Severity: BleepingComputer describes CVE-2026-118737 as a high-severity RCE; numeric CVSS **not specified by source** in the coverage read. Microsoft's November updates are also still causing activation failures and SQL Server performance issues, with a complex-formatting printing bug under investigation.
- *Why it matters:* the workaround is a deliberate rollback of exactly the relay protection the patch exists to add. Teams under holiday pressure will be tempted to set the registry key fleet-wide rather than per affected host, which re-opens NTLM relay exposure during a month when exploitation has been fast-moving.
- *Action:* scope `ClientAllowUnsignedNtlm` to the specific affected clients only, inventory proxies/SSO/packaging products that speak NTLM and confirm Kerberos support, and treat "Not Found" Target Machine log entries as your detection signal.

Source: BleepingComputer, "Microsoft confirms authentication issues after November updates," Wed 2026-11-25 04:02 EST, https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/microsoft/microsoft-confirms-authentication-issues-after-november-updates/

## Attacks and campaigns

**Treasury's FinCEN takes systems offline after "unauthorized access"; BSA filing deadlines extended** — Event date: notice posted Tuesday evening, **Nov 24** (The Record published 00:00 GMT Nov 25).

A banner on FinCEN's Bank Secrecy Act E-Filing site says the bureau is "investigating a cybersecurity incident" that "resulted in unauthorized access to a limited subset of its systems." As a proactive measure FinCEN took some systems offline; the BSA E-Filing website remains operational but "some services may be temporarily unavailable," and the agency is extending filing deadlines for affected filers and directing institutions that must send suspicious activity reports to law enforcement to use the Financial Institutions Hotline. No group has claimed it and no scope, vector or data-loss statement has been published; Treasury and FinCEN did not answer The Record's requests for comment. The context is heavy: Chinese state-backed actors (Silk Typhoon) breached Treasury offices in 2024–2025 — including systems used by the then-Secretary and Deputy Secretary, OFAC and the Office of Financial Research — and twelve Chinese nationals, two of them Ministry of Public Security officers, were charged over the 2024 intrusion.
- *Why it matters:* FinCEN is the U.S. financial intelligence unit — SAR/CTR intake is precisely the system that must keep working, and the disruption lands in a reduced-staffing holiday week for both the agency and the reporting institutions that depend on it.
- *Action:* if you file via BSA E-Filing, confirm your submission status, use the extension language FinCEN published, and route urgent SAR referrals through the Financial Institutions Hotline; expect and document deadline-relief communications.

**CIA, ODNI and NGA breached on a shared unclassified OSINT analysis network; ARTEX reportedly used** — Event date: first reported Tuesday evening **Nov 24** (New York Times); confirmed by Nextgov at 17:46 EST; BleepingComputer Nov 25 02:45 EST.

The CIA and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence discovered a breach of an unclassified system used to analyze public and commercially available information (open-source intelligence), and The New York Times also reported the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency was affected. A second person said the intrusion disrupted an unclassified network but did not touch classified systems and has been resolved; ODNI told CNN there were "no disruptions to critical capabilities or classified systems." A CIA official told the Washington Examiner the affected network was disrupted and the issue resolved. Reporting from the Times and Nextgov says the intrusion was carried out with **ARTEX**, the AI-driven penetration-testing/autonomous-intrusion framework associated with the Chinese engineer "Autumn-27," which has also been tied to recent federal and commercial intrusions; Nextgov additionally reports ARTEX was used in a low-impact intrusion into the Defense Department's Cyber Defense Command (DCDC). What was viewed or copied is unknown. Analysts quoted in the coverage (Huntress, SecurityScorecard) note the exposure is about method and priorities: query history, tracked subjects and trusted sources reveal what U.S. analysts are watching and where their gaps are, without touching a classified file. No link has been established to the FBI's NCIC incident or the USDA farmer-payment outage.
- *Why it matters:* an OSINT workspace is an aggregation of judgment, not just public data — it is a target that maps U.S. intelligence priorities. It is also the highest-profile confirmation yet that agentic tooling is operating inside U.S. government networks, alongside a federal incident streak that now includes the FBI, USDA, FAA comms and the NSA's classified network.
- *Action:* for agencies and vendors supporting IC/Gov workloads, re-baseline unclassified shared platforms (MFA phishing-resistance, external-facing analytics tooling, supply-chain access), and expect the ARTEX detection guidance (FBI FLASH of Nov 13, signature pack of Nov 13) to be applied to unclassified estates.
