## Vulnerabilities and patches

### BlockStrap: unauthenticated RCE in Adobe Commerce and Magento (CVE-2026-103849)

Event date: Adobe published the patch October 13, 2026; Sansec's public analysis followed October 15, 2026; reported October 16, 2026.

Severity: CVSS 10.0 (Sansec); BleepingComputer describes it as maximum-severity.

Summary: Sansec's BlockStrap research describes a second-pass template-engine injection in Adobe Commerce and Magento Open Source through version 2.4.9 and Adobe Commerce B2B through 1.5.3, letting an unauthenticated attacker run arbitrary PHP on the store server. Adobe fixed it in bulletin APSB26-153 on October 13, but Magento Open Source stores on versions 2.4.4-p18, 2.4.5-p17, and 2.4.6-p15 need an Adobe Commerce license for the patch or must upgrade to 2.4.7-p10 or later. Sansec reproduced four exploit routes within twenty-four hours of the patch, has observed no live exploitation yet, and warns that an AI agent can reverse-engineer the patch into a working exploit quickly.

Why it matters: Adobe rated this CVSS 10.0 flaw priority 2 (patch within thirty days), reserving priority 1 for vulnerabilities known to be exploited. With the patch public and no in-the-wild attacks yet, the thirty-day window gives attackers time to weaponize a flaw in internet-facing e-commerce servers.

Action: Sansec urges merchants to patch now instead of waiting thirty days — apply the 2026-oct release or Adobe's isolated patch, and keep the September StyleSmuggler hotfix (VULN-39341) in place.

### BeyondTrust patches actively exploited Remote Support zero-day (CVE-2026-102764)

Event date: exploitation since late August 2026; patch released to SaaS customers October 8, 2026; public warning Thursday, October 15, 2026; reported October 16, 2026.

Severity: CVSS 9.3 (The Hacker News); BleepingComputer describes it as critical.

Summary: BeyondTrust warned customers to patch an authentication-bypass flaw in Remote Support (formerly Bomgar) versions 26.2.3 and earlier that lets unprivileged remote attackers perform file operations on the appliance. Mandiant and Google Threat Intelligence Group track the exploiting espionage actor as Armory Castle (formerly UNC5221; CrowdStrike tracks it as Warp Panda). Attackers gained initial access, escalated to root using SUID permissions, deployed Sawfly web shell and Bareclaw tunneler malware, wiped logs, and moved laterally with stolen Administrator credentials. SaaS deployments were patched October 8 and fully patched by October 9; self-hosted customers must apply the patch manually. Shadowserver tracks over 1,400 BeyondTrust remote support instances exposed online.

Why it matters: Remote Support appliances sit at the network edge and broker credentials for customers including 75 percent of the Fortune 100 and government agencies. Mandiant says any appliance reachable from the internet on a vulnerable version should be treated as potentially compromised until reviewed, and the actor has a history of racing to exploit more targets after public disclosure.

Action: BeyondTrust recommends self-hosted Privileged Remote Access customers apply the patch immediately. Mandiant advises treating internet-reachable vulnerable appliances as potentially compromised.

### Microsoft fixes about two dozen remote code execution flaws in Office

Event date: announced Thursday, October 15, 2026; reported October 16, 2026.

Severity: Not specified by source (described as high-severity; no CVSS published in coverage).

Summary: Hot on the heels of the record October Patch Tuesday rollout (1,413 vulnerabilities), Microsoft announced patches for over thirty new vulnerabilities, most of them in Office. About two dozen are high-severity remote code execution flaws, half of them targetable through Outlook's Preview Pane; exploitation generally requires tricking a recipient into opening a specially crafted document. Word received the majority of patches, with fewer for Excel, Outlook, PowerPoint, and SharePoint. Microsoft said none of the newly resolved issues are known to have been exploited in the wild as zero-days. The round also included a denial-of-service fix in ASP.NET Core and an elevation-of-privilege fix in Identity Web.

Why it matters: Office documents remain the most common initial-access vector in phishing intrusions, and Preview Pane bugs can fire without the user opening anything, so enterprise Office patching is urgent even with no known in-the-wild exploitation yet.

Action: Microsoft recommends installing all Office updates that apply to you.

### Zero-click Candiru spyware delivered through targeted ads at Al Jazeera ("AdFection")

Event date: attacks targeted at least 45 Al Jazeera devices between 2024 and 2025; public disclosure reported October 15–16, 2026.

Severity: Not specified by source (spyware campaign).

Summary: Citizen Lab, working with Al Jazeera, disclosed that Candiru spyware was delivered to Al Jazeera Media Network devices via targeted online advertisements in a zero-click attack requiring only that the malicious ad be rendered. Dubbed AdFection, the operation used Israeli advertising firm Discover-Tech as a bridge between mercenary spyware and the ad ecosystem: Discover-Tech bought placements through mainstream ad networks and exchanges, embedded JavaScript that contacted a "NOAS" targeting server, and served exploit code only to profiled targets. Citizen Lab estimates hundreds of thousands to millions of users were directed to the domains; ads carrying attacker script tags appeared across 75,373 mobile apps and 120,768 websites, and Discover-Tech won 442 million of 3.77 billion ad bids between March and June 2026. After Citizen Lab's reports, Google, IAB Europe, Beeswax, Eskimi, and Vungle removed offending accounts and blocked access to the domains.

Why it matters: This is the first public technical confirmation of mercenary spyware delivered through targeted advertising, demonstrating that ad infrastructure can silently reach media, government, and national-security networks at scale.

Action: None stated by the sources; the implicated advertising networks removed accounts and blocked domains after notification.

### Burger King Japan discloses breach of 11.18 million app member records

Event date: unauthorized access to the app membership server between October 2 and 7, 2026; follow-up investigation concluded October 13, 2026; disclosure press release Thursday, October 15, 2026; reported October 16, 2026.

Severity: Not specified by source.

Summary: Burger King Japan disclosed that attackers accessed the server managing its official smartphone app's member information, exposing roughly 11.18 million records including names, dates of birth, gender, email addresses, phone numbers, and order history. The company told Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission that the server stored no credit card numbers and it has found no evidence of credential access or secondary damage such as fraud. Affected customers were warned to watch for suspicious emails, SMS messages, and phone calls.

Why it matters: This is the latest in a wave of breaches at major Japanese companies this year, which researchers tie to attackers probing access-control, configuration, and authentication weaknesses and exploiting known vulnerabilities.

Action: Burger King Japan advises customers to ignore suspicious messages and never enter member information or passwords in response to them.

### Unit 42 uncovers PEERSTITCH memory-resident implant on Fortinet devices

Event date: intrusion cluster CL-STA-0954 (UNC5820) began September 2024; research disclosed October 16, 2026.

Severity: Not specified by source for the implant; the exploited FortiOS flaw, CVE-2025-61624, carries CVSS 5.4.

Summary: Palo Alto Networks Unit 42 disclosed PEERSTITCH, a previously unreported memory-resident ARM64 implant deployed on Fortinet FortiOS devices through CVE-2025-61624, a path-traversal flaw in the FortiOS command-line interpreter that Fortinet patched in April 2026. The broader operation began with CVE-2024-47575 (FortiJump) against a FortiManager appliance at a telecommunications managed service provider, from which attackers exfiltrated configurations, credentials, and private keys for more than 500 managed FortiGate firewalls. PEERSTITCH injects code into the miglogd and iked daemons to create a covert inter-process communication bridge, leaves nothing on disk, opens no listening ports, and initiates no outbound connections; attackers also abused Apache mod_md to spawn an SSH-based reverse shell.

Why it matters: Edge management appliances are force multipliers — one compromised FortiManager exposed the trust material of hundreds of downstream firewalls belonging to unrelated organizations, and patching the appliance does not invalidate the stolen keys and certificates.

Action: Unit 42 advises treating FortiManager credentials and certificates as compromised the moment the platform is breached, and rotating trust material on every managed device; patching alone is not enough.

## Defensive tools and advisories

### CISA adds exploited Atlassian flaw CVE-2026-21589 to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog

Event date: CISA added the vulnerability to its KEV catalog October 15, 2026; exploitation began in early October 2026; Atlassian advisory October 5, 2026; federal fix deadline October 18, 2026.

Severity: CVSS 9.3.

Summary: CISA added CVE-2026-21589, a critical arbitrary file access flaw in Atlassian Data Center products (Bitbucket, Confluence, Jira Service Management, Jira Software, Bamboo, Crowd, Crucible, and Fisheye), to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog. An unauthenticated attacker who knows the exact name and path of a file under the web application root can read it. watchTowr Labs published a technical analysis on October 6 showing double-colon path traversal (for example, ..::..::WEB::web.xml) that exposed crowd.properties credentials and allowed escalation to the jira-administrators group. Under Binding Operational Directive 22-01, federal civilian agencies must address the flaw by October 18, 2026. Atlassian says its Cloud products are patched and show no evidence of exploitation.

Why it matters: Arbitrary file access on collaboration platforms commonly leads to credential theft and full administrative takeover, and the KEV addition carries a binding federal deadline with confirmed active exploitation.

Action: CISA orders federal agencies to fix the flaw by October 18, 2026; private organizations should inventory and patch affected Atlassian Data Center versions. Atlassian Cloud customers need no action.
