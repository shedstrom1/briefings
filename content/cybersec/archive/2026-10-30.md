## Vulnerabilities and patches

### Microsoft finally resolves the September-updates Always On VPN failures
**Event date:** Friday, October 30, 2026 (7:20 AM ET) — update on the Oct 14 fix claim Microsoft retracted Oct 20.
**Severity:** Not specified by source (service-affecting bug, not a security CVE).
Microsoft has resolved the Always On VPN connection failures introduced by the September 2026 Windows 11 security updates on Windows 11 24H2 and later. Connections configured for automatic protocol selection (IKEv2/SSTP fallback) could hang in "Connecting" or fail with "The specified port is already in use." Microsoft's first claimed fix in KB5127648/KB5127646 (Oct 14) was retracted after it did not work — an item this brief footnoted on Oct 20 — and the single-protocol SSTP/IKEv2-only workaround was the only stopgap until today.
**Why it matters:** Always On VPN replaced DirectAccess as the default enterprise remote-access path; a months-long regression in it is exactly the kind of patch-quality erosion that undermines emergency-patch discipline during exploitation storms like PHP2Shell.
**Action:** Install the current October 2026 cumulative updates; the temporary mitigation (pin the VPN profile to SSTP-only or IKEv2-only) is no longer needed once patched.

## Attacks and campaigns

### Binance links $6.3B crypto theft to a compromised DevOps laptop; TRM and Chainalysis blame North Korea's TraderTraitor
**Event date:** Thursday, October 29, 2026 (initial report 6:45 PM ET; Binance confirmation and TRM attribution through early Oct 30).
The world's largest exchange confirmed that roughly $6.36B was moved out of its wallets across ten blockchains — most of it in about 20 minutes starting 20:05 UTC Oct 29 — after an attacker accessed a service container via a DevOps engineer's compromised laptop and submitted unauthorized withdrawals. Binance found "no evidence of a wallet private key leak," says it will cover all losses from its SAFU fund, and suspended deposits/withdrawals while "uncovering additional points of breach." Tether, Circle, Ripple and World Liberty Financial froze ~$2.67B in stablecoins and Arbitrum's Security Council froze ~$30.5M, but the attacker raced the freezes — selling USDT to market makers and borrowing ETH against USDT on Aave — and still holds 420,000+ ETH and 18,270 BTC (~$2.4B) beyond any issuer's reach. TRM Labs called it "the largest crypto theft on record, about four times the $1.5B Bybit hack"; Chainalysis agreed "with a high degree of confidence" it is TraderTraitor (Lazarus/APT38).
**Why it matters:** If the hot-wallet signing path was reached through a DevOps endpoint, the industry's "cold storage + key rotation" playbook has another gap — privileged build/deploy access, not seed phrases. For enterprises, it is a reminder that the vendor's *engineers*, not just the vendor's services, are the breach vector; Binance says the attack "was not a single one-click action."
**Action:** Binance users — no action required on funds (exchange says assets are safe and will publish updated Proof of Reserves); treat any "Binance compensation" email or DM as phishing until Binance posts on its own channels.

### VAPOR PANDA used ARTEX agentic AI with DeepSeek against 200+ organizations in 11 days
**Event date:** Thursday, October 29, 2026 (CrowdStrike blog; NBC exclusive 6:00 PM ET).
CrowdStrike OverWatch says China-nexus group VAPOR PANDA ran the ARTEX agent framework — alongside the Chinese-developed DeepSeek LLM plus an unconfirmed second commercial LLM — from a single controller against 200+ organizations in at least 14 countries from October 17. Confirmed post-exploitation at nine organizations (foreign ministries of two Latin American countries, likely a third; a Middle East interior ministry; a European country's presidency, PM office, defense/justice/finance/intelligence entities; 19 US pharma/semiconductor/defense/nuclear/financial targets; telecoms in the US, Belgium and the Philippines), with the BLOODALCHEMY backdoor deployed and credential spraying after access. Planning agents consumed ~1.4 billion input tokens in a single intrusion; transcripts show a human operator steering agents in Chinese (【人类指令】 labels, China Standard Time timestamps), including instructions to inventory endpoint sensor coverage — i.e., built-in CrowdStrike-sensor evasion. CrowdStrike stresses this campaign is separate from the Storm-0940/ConnectWise ARTEX activity reported Oct 21.
**Why it matters:** This is the first well-documented case of a state actor running *parallel, multi-target* agentic intrusions with humans only at decision points — scale that previously required whole teams. The sensor-inventory step means attackers are teaching agents where your EDR isn't.
**Action:** Sweep for BLOODALCHEMY and Chisel/SoftEther tunneling; audit unsigned SAML assertions and unrotated mail-server admin keys (both exploited here); verify Falcon sensor coverage on every host so "sensor-less" hosts don't become the agents' preferred entry.
Source: CrowdStrike Blog, "VAPOR PANDA Uses AI-Driven ARTEX to Grow Global Espionage Campaign," Oct 29, 2026 — https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/blog/vapor-panda-uses-artex-to-scale-global-campaign/
Source: NBC News (Kevin Collier), "Chinese hackers used DeepSeek and other AI tools in sweeping global cyberattacks, report finds," Oct 29, 2026 6:00 PM EDT — https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/security/china-linked-hackers-used-ai-target-200-organizations-crowdstrike-says-rcna606343

### Official Claude Code extension trojanized on Cursor Marketplace with persistent SSH backdoor
**Event date:** Thursday, October 29, 2026 (malicious build live 2:27 AM–1:35 PM PT; Cursor security notice same day; THN follow Oct 30).
A maliciously modified `anthropic.claude-code` version 2.1.313 was served to at most 5,000 users from the Cursor VS Code extension Marketplace. A 66-line loader inserted before the legitimate bundle collects hostname, username, OS/arch, home/working dirs, global git email, public IP, and the *names* (not values) of up to 40 environment variables matching KEY/TOKEN/SECRET/PASS/AWS/GCP; appends an attacker SSH key labeled `svc-telemetry` to authorized_keys; attempts to enable SSH and open a reverse tunnel to 134[.]209[.]82[.]225; and persists via Windows scheduled task "SysMon" (SYSTEM) / macOS LaunchAgent masquerading as `com.apple.sysmond`, beaconing to port 8099/beacon over HTTP. Cursor (SpaceXAI) shut the marketplace down, rotated keys, rebuilt it, worked with the hosting provider to kill the C2 ("the attacker server no longer functions"), and says enabling the local SSH service failed without admin rights on most hosts — so attacker login via the tunnel likely didn't succeed, though the key, persistence and recon still landed. Anthropic shipped clean 2.1.314 to Open VSX and the Microsoft marketplace by Oct 30. Full IOCs, checks (`grep -qs svc-telemetry ~/.ssh/authorized_keys` and the PowerShell equivalent) and remediation scripts are published.
**Why it matters:** The compromise was of the *marketplace distribution*, not Anthropic's repo — auto-update users could have consumed the malware with no Anthropic-side signal. A week after the OVHcloud CLI release hijack, IDE/marketplace trust chains are now the repeat supply-chain kill path for AI tooling.
**Action:** Anyone who installed/updated `anthropic.claude-code` 2.1.313 from cursor.com/marketplace between 2:27 AM–1:35 PM PT Oct 29: run Cursor's check, isolate hosts showing execution, remove the svc-telemetry key + persistence (uninstalling the extension alone is not enough — standalone persistence scripts keep running), rebuild if in doubt, and rotate credentials/sessions from a trusted system.
Source: Cursor Security Notice, "Security notice — malicious version of Anthropic.claude-code (2.1.313) identified in the Cursor Extension Marketplace," Oct 29, 2026 — https://cursor.com/blog/claude-code-extension-security-notice
Source: The Hacker News (Ravie Lakshmanan), "Official Claude Code Extension on Cursor Marketplace Trojanized with Persistent SSH Backdoor," Oct 30, 2026 — https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/official-claude-code-vs-code-extension.html

### Polymarket halts trading after DebugBear RUM script weaponized into a crypto drainer; ~$2.5M traced
**Event date:** Thursday, October 29, 2026 (exchange went cancel-only 2:05 PM ET; resolved 3:05 PM ET; BleepingComputer 5:47 PM ET).
Attackers compromised DebugBear — a website performance-monitoring (Real User Monitoring) service — and pushed malicious JavaScript from `cdn.debugbear[.]com/C7xcKKrjUnXK.js` onto Polymarket's international site. The script detected logged-in sessions, liquidated users' prediction-market positions and converted/transferred assets to attacker wallet 0x3F99...fc18 using Polymarket's own transaction functionality; ~28.6M POL (~$2.57M) was moved on to three more wallets, with activity clustering 1:50–1:56 PM ET. `data.debugbear.com` served as C2/exfil for wallet addresses, balances and results. Polymarket fixed it in under an hour (a production build removed the DebugBear integration at 1:59 PM ET), referred the breach to law enforcement, and pledged full reimbursement. DebugBear confirmed "an ongoing security incident" and told all customers to remove its embed code; its own site was down during the response.
**Why it matters:** A third-party analytics tag is full JavaScript trust — the attackers turned the victim's own trading API against its users without stealing a single password or seed phrase. Any site running RUM/A/B/consent tags inherits its vendor's breach.
**Action:** Any site embedding third-party monitoring JavaScript should audit script inventories and pin/SRI-check where possible, and follow DebugBear's instruction to remove its embed until it declares containment; Polymarket users: verify wallet activity, take up the reimbursement offer.
Source: BleepingComputer (Lawrence Abrams), "Polymarket halts trading after crypto-draining script breach, $2.5M traced," Oct 29, 2026 — https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/polymarket-halts-trading-after-crypto-draining-script-breach-25m-traced/
Source: SecurityWeek, "$2 Million+ Drained From Polymarket Traders in New Supply Chain Attack," Oct 29–30, 2026 — https://www.securityweek.com/2-million-drained-from-polymarket-traders-in-new-supply-chain-attack/

### Hugging Face GPU-mining campaign returns within days of takedown; 31 repos, 32,000+ downloads
**Event date:** Thursday, October 29, 2026 (Pluto Security report; BleepingComputer 6:09 PM ET).
Pluto Security tracked a cryptomining/backdoor campaign distributing 31 malicious repositories across seven Hugging Face accounts — fake "Qwen2 test checkpoints" that abuse the `trust_remote_code` option to run arbitrary Python at model-load time. The flagship model (MENXIANGYU/qwen2-tiny, 28,000+ downloads) contained no model at all: it installed a miner disguised as a Linux system process, killed competing miners, and opened reverse shells. After Pluto reported the first wave (removed by HF and ModelScope), 19 second-wave repos appeared within days under new accounts and were still live eight days later; one was uploaded hours after the report and included a container-escape script. The operator — assessed as financially motivated, Chinese-speaking — has taken ~$5,000 in Pearl (PRL) on-chain, and nine machines in a shared research GPU cluster began mining for the operator while researchers watched. Separately, Air Security flagged a GitHub "solana-starter-kit" repo (286 stars, README-only) positioned to be the top pick for AI coding agents and get them to run a NetSupport RAT installer.
**Why it matters:** HF's AV-based scanning "scanned clean" a repo with no model, just attacker code — hub scanning checks known malware, not what a model's Python actually does. The AI-agent README lure extends the same trust-abuse from humans to autonomous coding agents.
**Action:** Treat any host that loaded third-party models with `trust_remote_code=True` as compromised — validate against Pluto's IOCs, rotate reachable credentials, and remember killing the miner does not remove the backdoors. Use `trust_remote_code=False`/allow-listed code review for models from unknown authors.
Source: BleepingComputer (Ax Sharma), "Hugging Face malware campaign returns to hijack GPUs for crypto mining," Oct 29, 2026 06:09 PM ET — https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hugging-face-malware-campaign-returns-to-hijack-gpus-for-crypto-mining/
