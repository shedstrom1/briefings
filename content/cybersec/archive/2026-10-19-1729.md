## Vulnerabilities and patches

**Siemens confirms in-the-wild exploitation of Software Center DLL side-loading flaw (CVE-2026-110936)**
*Event date: Monday, October 19, 2026 (Siemens advisory update + blog post; story published 2:15 PM ET)*
Siemens disclosed on Monday that threat actors are exploiting CVE-2026-110936, a DLL-search-path flaw in Siemens Software Center rated CVSS v4 8.4, as part of a malware campaign. The flaw (disclosed October 15, fixed in version 4.0.9.8, auto-applied on next launch) lets a local authenticated attacker plant a malicious DLL that Software Center loads with its own privileges. The observed attacks pair a genuine, validly signed copy of the program with attacker-supplied DLLs — no Siemens distribution channel was breached — and Siemens links the activity to Romulus, the multi-payload ClickFix delivery platform Forescout documented last week. Huntress's Jose Oregon is credited with reporting the bug.
*Severity as published:* CVSS v4 8.4 (Siemens).
**Why it matters:** Abusing a legitimately signed vendor binary sideloaded from its own folder defeats hash-based detection and lends false trust to a trusted engineering-tool brand; Siemens says defenders must hunt behavior and the malware's folders, not file hashes — and Huntress believes other vendors' signed programs are being recycled the same way.
**Action:** Ensure Software Center auto-updates to 4.0.9.8; hunt for Software Center binaries loading DLLs from user-writable folders and for ClickFix-style paste-and-run lures; use Siemens' published IoCs.
Source: SecurityWeek — "Siemens Software Center Vulnerability Exploited in ClickFix Malware Attacks," Eduard Kovacs, October 19, 2026 (2:15 PM ET) — https://www.securityweek.com/siemens-software-center-vulnerability-exploited-in-clickfix-malware-attacks/
Source: Siemens ProductCERT advisory SSA-365596 (DLL hijacking, Software Center / Solid Edge) — https://cert-portal.siemens.com/productcert/html/ssa-365596.html

**Update on previously reported — Plex2Shell chain weaponized hours after PoC: miners + "myawesomeproxy" on compromised Plex servers**
*Event date: exploitation observed October 17–19, 2026; coverage October 19 (ledger: footnoted Oct 17 as pre-window — this is the new-exploitation development)*
The four-CVE Plex Media Server chain dubbed Plex2Shell (CVE-2026-96652 SSRF, CVSS 5.3; CVE-2026-96654 admin-token leak, 6.9; CVE-2026-96655 SSRF, 5.3; CVE-2026-96656 path-control RCE endpoint, 8.6) is being actively exploited a day after its technical details went public October 16. Attackers from IPs including 91.92.40[.]202 and 176.65.139[.]228 chained the bugs to an unauthenticated RCE, pulling an XMRig miner ("mylovelyminer," mining pool at ota.proxystream[.]st — a domain infrastructure previously tied by Bitsight to the AISURU proxyware botnet) and a stealthy Linux reverse-proxy backdoor ("myawesomeproxy") from 217.60.194[.]116. The bugs were reported to Plex on July 27 and patched August 3 in Plex Media Server 1.43.3.10861.
*Severity as published:* chain peaks at CVSS 8.6 (CVE-2026-96656).
**Why it matters:** This is the familiar disclosure-to-weaponization gap compressed to ~36 hours on internet-exposed media servers that often sit unpatched for months — and the proxyware payload quietly resells victims' bandwidth, extending the blast radius beyond the server itself.
**Action:** Update Plex Media Server to ≥1.43.3.10861 now; check exposed instances for unexpected "mylovelyminer"/"myawesomeproxy" processes and the listed attacker IPs.

## Attacks and campaigns

**AOMEI Backupper update servers trojanized in "ephemeral tampering" supply-chain attacks; CROSSROAD/SHINYROAD backdoors delivered**
*Event date: research published Monday, October 19, 2026 (BC, 3:40 PM ET); attacks occurred in three windows May 28–July 20, 2026*
Researchers at ManageEngine's Threat Analysis Lab, working with Google's Threat Intelligence Group and Mandiant, documented a supply-chain campaign (attributed to UNC7017) in which attackers briefly modified two unsigned configuration files on AOMEI's own servers — down_html.ini (which tells Backupper where to pull update packages) and Notifycfg.ini (which can trigger content execution) — for 5–10 minute bursts on May 28, July 16 and July 20, 2026, then restored them to original state, even tampering with the expected checksum so the malicious ZIP passed verification. Clients polling during those windows received the previously unknown CROSSROAD downloader and the SHINYROAD backdoor, which loads malicious code through legitimate signed Radmin components and calls out to a C2 server; on July 20 the campaign also attempted NTLM-hash theft via a hidden WebDAV popup. Victims were identified in the US, Australia, India, Singapore, Spain and Switzerland across IT services, telecom and higher education, and Google warns the attacks were opportunistic, so any client that checked for updates during the bursts could be affected. AOMEI says it has secured its servers, but ManageEngine notes a server-side fix does not remove implants already delivered.
*Severity as published:* Not specified by source.
**Why it matters:** "Ephemeral tampering" — editing update configs for minutes at a time and reverting — defeats point-in-time integrity checks and forensic baselines on the vendor side, and targets backup software, a prime-positioned foothold for later ransomware staging.
**Action:** If you run AOMEI Backupper, review the Google/ManageEngine IoCs: suspicious scheduled tasks, unexpected executables in AOMEI installation directories, and connections to attacker-controlled infrastructure; assume compromise for clients that polled during the three exposure windows.
Source: BleepingComputer — "Hackers hijack AOMEI update servers to secretly spread malware," Lawrence Abrams, October 19, 2026 (3:40 PM ET) — https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hackers-hijack-aomei-update-servers-to-secretly-spread-malware/
Source: Google Cloud Threat Intelligence — "Investigating Ephemeral Supply Chain Tampering" (GTIG/Mandiant report) — https://cloud.google.com/blog/topics/threat-intelligence/investigating-ephemeral-supply-chain-tampering

**Bullhorn pulls staffing platform offline to contain cyberattack; ATS/CRM restored, data-theft question still open**
*Event date: company status updates and reporting Monday, October 19 (BC 2:51 PM ET; recovery update ~3:39 PM ET); cyber incident first detected Saturday, October 17*
Staffing-software vendor Bullhorn disabled access to its platform — ATS/CRM, Automation, Back Office, Talent Platform and sandboxes — to contain a cyberattack that caused a system-wide outage, saying it detected the incident Saturday and is working with CrowdStrike specialists. Through this afternoon the company said it had isolated the issue and eradicated the immediate threat, restored ATS/CRM in the Australia, EMEA/UK and US regions, and begun mandatory password resets, but had "not yet determined whether customer data was exfiltrated"; the company says the incident affected ATS/CRM with no indication so far to other products. Third-party integrators wired into Bullhorn's APIs reported write-backs and job-board syncs failing while the platform was dark.
*Severity as published:* Not specified by source.
**Why it matters:** Bullhorn is the system of record for staffing-firm candidate and client data; a containment blackout is a supply-chain outage for the whole staffing ecosystem (integration vendors, job boards, VMS syncs), and the forced credential/API-password rotation is exactly the cleanup wave attackers trigger when initial access mechanism is unknown.
**Action:** Staffing firms on Bullhorn: rotate integration API credentials you host, watch the status page for the forensics verdict, and open the support ticket Bullhorn requests; treat any inbound "Bullhorn" communications this week as suspect.
Source: BleepingComputer — "Bullhorn takes staffing platform offline to contain cyberattack," Bill Toulas, October 19, 2026 (2:51 PM ET) — https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/bullhorn-takes-staffing-platform-offline-to-contain-cyberattack/

**GhostAction escalates: fake-font credential stealer planted in 94 GitHub repos, "my-github.com" device-code phishing**
*Event date: disclosed by OpenSourceMalware; coverage Monday, October 19, 2026 (The Hacker News)*
The GhostAction supply-chain actor, known for injecting malicious GitHub Actions workflows, has expanded after GitHub blunted the workflow vector: it now phishes open-source maintainers via a lookalike domain (my-github[.]com, registered October 9 at IP 193.32.204[.]199 — the same exfiltration endpoint) with a "new login from Moscow" alert; the reverse-proxy login page harvests passwords and 2FA codes and silently auto-authorizes a GitHub OAuth device flow with repo, workflow and read:org scopes. A second-stage stealer has been planted in 94 repositories (66 owners): a launcher in `.vscode/tasks.json` executes a payload hidden as `public/fonts/fa-solid-400.woff2` — actually 38 lines of readable Node.js that vacuum up AWS, npm/PyPI, Git, Kubernetes and Claude/Gemini credential files plus environment variables. Two repos were re-infected even after owners removed earlier malicious workflows; OpenSourceMalware's Jenn Gile says the operator's sloppiness (single exfil IP, timeout-prone scanning) is what keeps exposing them.
*Severity as published:* Not specified by source.
**Why it matters:** The attack converts a developer's own VS Code launch (tasks.json auto-run) into code execution and an OAuth-consent bypass into persistent workflow write access — poisoning CI/CD from the developer workstation, and re-infecting "cleaned" repos means one-time remediation is not enough.
**Action:** Audit `.vscode/tasks.json` and `public/fonts/` in recently cloned repos; revoke GitHub tokens/OAuth apps from a clean device, rotate every credential workflows can reach, and alert users who cloned affected projects (IoCs incl. SHA-256 published by OpenSourceMalware).
Source: The Hacker News — "GhostAction Attackers Plant Credential Stealer Disguised as Font in 94 GitHub Repositories," Ravie Lakshmanan, October 19, 2026 — https://thehackernews.com/2026/10/ghostaction-attackers-plant-credential.html
Source: OpenSourceMalware — "GhostAction copycats PolinRider by hiding a stealer in a fake font file" — https://opensourcemalware.com/blog/ghostaction-copycats-polinrider-by-hiding-a-stealer-in-a-fake-font-file
